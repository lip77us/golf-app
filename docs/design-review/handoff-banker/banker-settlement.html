Banker, settling up

Unlike every other game in the app, Banker’s debts really are pairwise. Every bet was one named golfer against one named golfer for an agreed number, so the app knows exactly who owes whom — and the settlement can say so without inventing anything.

Which makes the collapse an explicit choice

Fifty-four true pairwise transactions net down to three handovers. The app shows the three, because nobody is settling a round with fifty-four exchanges of five dollar bills — but it keeps the itemisation one tap away, and unlike Sequoya 3s it can honestly say the detail is real rather than derived.

The receipt splits the round in two

Banking and betting are different games and the receipt says so before it says anything else: −$80 as banker over six holes, +$60 as a player over twelve. A golfer reads that pair and knows immediately what kind of day he had — and it is the only summary that survives being repeated at the bar.

Banked holes are itemised; played holes are grouped

The six holes he banked get a line each, with all three bets on them. He faced three opponents at once and that is where the money and the arguments live, so it is written out in full.

The twelve holes he merely played are grouped by whose bank he was betting into — seven holes against Sam, three against Dave, two against Lee — with a subtotal. Twelve individual five-dollar lines would bury the six that matter, and the grouping happens to answer the question a golfer actually asks: who took my money.

The hole that paid nobody

The 14th is on the receipt at zero, and it is the most instructive line on it: a par 3 where all three opponents tripled into a $180 hole, two of them lost, one made birdie, and the banker came out exactly level.

Rolling that line up or dropping it would be the natural instinct and the wrong one. A golfer who remembers a $180 hole and cannot find it on his receipt does not trust the receipt. Zero-value lines stay when the stake was large.

A tie pays nobody, and the receipt still shows it

The banker does not win ties. Match his net and the bet is void, whatever it had grown to — so a tied bet reads $0 tied with the amount it reached printed beside it. The 7th is the case: Paul countered into $40 apiece and then halved two of the three, collecting on neither. That is the receipt’s job — a golfer who remembers a $40 bet and cannot find it does not trust the receipt.

The old draft had ties paying the banker, and it was the rule most often forgotten by whoever was not banking — settlement is exactly where the forgetting produces an argument.

What is not on the receipt

Bets between the other two golfers on a hole he did not bank. Those were private and stay private, even in a four-man game where everybody was standing there. The app is not the group’s memory of what everyone else had on.

Still open

  • Whether the shortest payment set is the right default, or whether a group would rather pay each other in full pairwise — some do, on the grounds that it settles who beat whom.
  • Whether to offer a running tab for a regular game. More pressing here than in Sequoya 3s: Banker swings are large enough that a single Saturday can be an unpleasant number.
