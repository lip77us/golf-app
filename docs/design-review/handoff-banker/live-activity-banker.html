Banker on the lock screen

Of every game in the app this is the one with the strongest case for a Live Activity, and for a reason the others do not have: the thing you most need to know is a thing you were told once, on a tee box, twenty minutes ago.

Who is banking. What you bet. Whether somebody doubled it. All three were spoken aloud and none of them are written anywhere a golfer can check — which is exactly the gap a lock-screen card exists to fill.

This card is personal, and that is a departure

Sixes and Sequoya 3s put a neutral scoreboard on the lock screen — the same string on four phones, so two golfers can read one device. Banker cannot do that. There is no shared state on a Banker hole; there are three separate one-on-ones with three different numbers, and the score is not a thing that exists.

So the card reads from the holder’s side. The banker’s phone shows his exposure; a player’s phone shows his bet. Two compositions of one card, drawn side by side below, and the only place in the set where the same hole produces two different headlines.

Banker sees$90 at stake, the three bets itemised, and that he countered.
Player sees$40 at stake, who he is playing, and what multiplied it.
Neither seesAnyone else’s bet. A player has no business reading Sam’s number off Dave’s phone.

The headline is the hole, not the round

The running total goes in the footer with the stake, as everywhere else in the set. The 36-point number is what is live right now, because in this game that figure moves by a factor of four between the tee and the fairway and it is the only thing a glance cannot reconstruct.

A round total in the headline would be the safer choice and the wrong one: it is the number you already know, and it does not change while you are walking.

Two pushes, and the second one is the point

New bankerUp to seventeen a round. Gold. Names who banks and what he set.
Counter-doubleAmber. Your bet just doubled and you did not agree to it.
No pushYour own double. You called it — you know.

The counter-double push is the one that justifies the whole activity. It is the single event in this game where a golfer’s money changes without his consent or his knowledge, and it is legal, and it happens while three balls are in the air and nobody is watching a phone. Learning about it at settlement is how groups stop playing Banker.

Seventeen banker-change pushes is a lot, and it is the honest count — the role rotates every hole. Sending one per hole is defensible only because the hole is unplayable without it: you cannot choose a bet if you do not know whose max you are betting into. If field testing says it is too many, the fallback is a silent activity update and a push only when the banker changes his maximum from the last hole.

The state slot holds the role

BANKING in gold on the banker’s phone; V. PAUL on a player’s. Gold appears nowhere else in the set and does one job here: it marks the role. Mint stays the app’s colour and never means banker.

Eighteen bankers, in the island

The expanded island carries a strip of eighteen — gold where you banked. That is the one summary this format produces that nothing else does: how many holes you held the role, which correlates with both your scoring and your variance, and which no golfer can count from memory.

Who strokes, in the ribbon

Every bet on a Banker hole settles on net, so a golfer cannot read his own one-on-one without knowing who gets a shot — and this is the one fact on the card he cannot recover by looking at the group. Survivor’s ribbon slot carries it: a strip above the header naming the strokes on this hole, with the stroke index it came from on the trailing edge.

It is blue, not gold. Gold means the role and nothing else in this card; strokes are a neutral fact of the hole, not an alarm, so they take the one colour the set does not otherwise use.

Banker seesWhich of the three opponents strokes, and whether he does — each bet line carries a dot ahead of the name, the same mark the score-entry screen uses.
Player seesOnly the two men in his bet: himself and the banker. Whether Sam gets a shot has no bearing on his money.
Nobody seesA stroke count for the round. Gets 4 is an eighteen-hole fact and belongs on the card, not here.

When neither man in the bet strokes, the ribbon reads scratch hole rather than disappearing — an absent ribbon is indistinguishable from a card that forgot.

The final state names the swing

The money, the gross, and the hole that decided it. Banker rounds are usually settled by one or two holes where a counter-double met three good shots, and the golfer already half-remembers which one — the card finishes the sentence.

Still open

  • Whether the banker’s headline should be his exposure (drawn) or his net position on the hole once scores start landing. The second is more useful late in a hole and undefined early.
  • Whether a player should be able to see the total on the hole without seeing the split. Probably yes — it is the banker’s risk, not a private number — but it invites arithmetic.
  • Whether the seventeen rotation pushes survive contact with a real round.
