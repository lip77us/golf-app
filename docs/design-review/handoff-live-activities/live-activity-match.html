Match play on the lock screen

Singles and fourball are one card. Both are a single match between two sides over eighteen holes, decided by holes won, and every slot answers the same question in both. The only difference is how many names sit on a side — one, or two joined by an ampersand.

That is why they were not drawn separately. Two cards that differ by a conjunction would drift apart within a release, and there is nothing in fourball’s shape that singles does not have.

This card never pushes

The push test is could the reader have known this from the hole they are standing on, and in a two-side match played inside one group the answer is always yes. Both sides are within earshot of every putt. A hole won, a match going dormie, a match closing out on the 15th — the reader watched all of it happen.

So match play earns an activity and no notifications at all. Sixes pushes because the pairing is drawn; Nassau pushes because a press is a decision somebody else made. Neither exists here. A card with nothing unknowable on it is the honest case for ambient-only, and it is worth stating plainly so nobody adds Dave won the 7th later.

ActivityYes. It is the running state of one match.
NotificationNone, all round. Nothing in a one-group match is news.
ConsequenceThe card must be correct at a glance without ever having announced itself.

Sides are fixed for the round, so colour can be too

Blue and orange are assigned once and hold to the 18th. Sixes has to re-assign them every match because the pairings are redrawn; a single match never changes sides, which makes the colour a reliable handle for the whole round — the blue number belongs to the blue name, hole 1 to hole 18.

The leading side takes the colour on both the number and the name; the trailing side is dimmed. ALL SQ is white at 90%, the same as Sixes and Nassau: mint is the app’s colour and cannot name a side.

The state slot holds the match word

DORMIE when the lead equals the holes left, n TO PLAY the rest of the time. Holes remaining is counted against the eighteen, not a segment — there are no segments here, which makes it simpler than Sixes and removes the nominal-range problem entirely.

Handicap is not on the card. Strokes given are fixed at the first tee and never change, which makes them a setup fact rather than news, and gets 4 beside a running match state invites subtracting it again. The hole-by-hole allocation is already in the app on a card that can afford it.

A match that closes early keeps the activity

This is the state the format actually needs and the one a naive card gets wrong. A match won 4 & 3 ends on the 15th, and the group has three holes left to play.

The card does not dismiss and does not freeze. The big number becomes the result — 4 & 3 in the winner’s colour — the state slot says CLOSED and names the hole it happened on, and the money slot finally has something in it, because a match pays when it closes and this one just did.

Then it holds that state and goes quiet for the remaining holes. The header keeps advancing, because the group is still playing golf and the reader’s gross is still moving, but nothing about the match changes again. A settled result is the correct thing for a card to be showing when the thing it reports is over.

Nothing takes over the live line. There is no second bet on the leftover holes in this format — a press is Nassau’s object, and it presses a nine that is still running rather than opening a new match on the tail of a finished one.

Money is empty for most of the round

Settled money only, and a single match settles exactly once. So on a straight singles match the money slot is empty from the 1st to the 17th and holds one figure at the end. That is correct rather than broken: an in-progress match is worth nothing yet, and $0 would read as a match played for nothing.

It is also the argument for the footer’s other two slots. Gross leads — the number a golfer checks without meaning to — then thru, then the stake, which never goes stale.

A no-stake round has no footer

Casual match play is often played for nothing. When there is no stake the footer is removed, not emptied: gross and thru would be left rattling around a row built to end in money, and a footer whose right edge is permanently blank looks like a bug for eighteen holes.

The card is three slots then — header, number, state — and it is better for it. Gross and thru move up beside the hole in the header, which had room: HOLE 5 · +2 THRU 4.

The score does not go with the footer. Gross against par is on every state of every card in this set, stake or no stake, because it is the number a golfer checks without meaning to and the only personal figure a neutral board carries. What the no-stake round removes is the money and the row built to end in it — not the reader’s round.

The one reader with no gross is a watcher, who is not playing. Skins is the card that has watchers today, and it trades the slot for par and the field size rather than leaving it blank.

The quiet treatment follows the same rule by the same route. It drops the footer to pull the card back, so gross and thru ride in the header beside the hole — HOLE 16 · +9 THRU 15. What the quiet version gives up is the stake and the money, which is the loud part; the score stays, because a state a golfer cannot read his own round off is not worth leaving running.

Fourball names four golfers and reports one ball

The sides line carries both pairings in full, the same as Sixes: Paul Kelly & Dave Moran against Sam Reid & Lee Naylor. Two Pauls in a group is common and initials do not settle it.

Whose ball counted is not on the card. It is the most tempting line here and it fails the push test twice over — the reader saw the hole, and it is history rather than state. The footer’s gross stays the reader’s own score, not the team’s better ball, because gross is the one personal figure the neutral board carries and a team figure under a neutral board is a number nobody can check against their card.

Always-on, and the final state

Same composition held back — the big number is the only thing that has to survive it. On the round signing, the neutral board becomes the one personal thing: what you won and who to see, held about five minutes, then dismissed.

Open questions

Concession. A conceded match is a result nobody scored, so it has no hole to attach to. Drawn as an ordinary close — 2 & 1, CLOSED — which loses the fact that it was given. If a concession should read differently, it is the one thing on this card that might earn a push, since a golfer in the group behind cannot see it.

Halved matches. Drawn as ALL SQ / HALVED with the money slot left empty. Confirm the stake is voided rather than banked at zero.

Presses in singles. Nassau owns the press today, and there is no code for one on a straight eighteen-hole match. If a casual singles match can be pressed, this card needs Nassau’s orange chip and its push, and the two cards start to converge.

Extra holes. A match all square after 18 that plays on has no hole number left in the header. HOLE 19 is drawn nowhere yet.
