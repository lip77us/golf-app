Skins on the lock screen

Skins gets the one big number back. Nassau needed two rows because it always has two matches live; skins has one thing that pays — the hole you are standing on — so the composition Sixes was built on returns intact.

The number is what this hole is worth. Not your position, not the leaderboard: the pot in front of you, which is the only figure that changes how the next tee shot gets played.

Only when skins is the primary game

A round gets one Live Activity and it belongs to the primary game, named at setup. So this card ships when skins is what the group came to play — and a foursome running skins alongside a Nassau gets the Nassau card and no skins card at all.

Worth stating because it closes a question the pattern left open: there is no ranking of games by newsworthiness and nothing to arbitrate at runtime. The group already answered it. Side games settle in the app like everything else.

The money is in the headline, and this is the exception

Both earlier activities pushed money to the footer, because a lock screen is a neutral board two golfers should be able to read and personal money in the headline breaks that.

Skins is the one game where the pot is not personal. Everyone on the tee is playing for the same $18. So it can hold the headline without breaking the rule — and because there are no sides, mint is free to carry it, which it never was in Sixes or Nassau where mint was the app’s colour and could not be mistaken for a team’s.

Nobody is named

The sides slot is empty and stays empty. In skins the field is the opponent, and naming it would be a list — the thing a lock screen has least room for.

What the slot holds instead is where the pot came from: 3 skins, carried from the 10th. That is the story of the hole and it is the reason the number is big.

The pot is provisional, and the card has to say so

This is the structural problem skins has and the other games do not. A skin is not decided until every group has played that hole. Your group can tie the 10th and think it carries, while a group an hour ahead already won it.

So the pot on your current hole is a forecast, not a fact, and the card marks it: the number goes white instead of mint, the state slot reads PROVISIONAL, and beneath it 2 GROUPS OUT — how many are still to play the holes in the carry.

When the field is through and nothing is outstanding, the number turns mint and the state reads ALL IN. Same slot, two truths, and the colour does the work at a glance.

Two consequences. Pool mode is always mint — the number there is the pool, which is fixed by the ante at setup and cannot be provisional; what is uncertain in pool is your share, and falling carries that. And the quiet treatment keeps the mint on the number even though it pulls it off everything else: the colour is load-bearing here, not decoration, and a quiet card that cannot distinguish a real pot from a guess has lost the one thing this card is for.

The news is a skin settling behind you

The 10th went to Sam Reid. A group ahead won a hole your group tied, so the carry you were counting on breaks — this hole is $12, not $18. Money moved while you were standing somewhere else, and no amount of watching your own card would have told you.

That is the exact test the other activities used, and skins passes it more cleanly than either: it is the only game here where the thing in front of you can change without you doing anything.

ActivityAmbient. The pot on this hole, always current.
NotificationPunctual. Fires when a skin settles behind you and changes the carry.
Never bothThe activity does not flash. It already shows the corrected pot.

Not every carry. A hole carrying is normal, expected, and already on the card — pushing it would be eighteen notifications. Only a settlement that moves the number in front of you is news.

Gross leads the footer

+2 · $2 a player · 6 settled. Same rule as the other two cards: gross is the number a golfer checks without meaning to, and the footer is already the row where the reader’s own round lives.

It earns its place here twice over, because par is in the header and gross is the other half of that sentence. Net skins turn on strokes, so par 4 and +2 together tell the reader what the last eleven holes have done to his chances on this one.

The money beside it is net, and settled

+$4 after six skins: two collected at $6, four paid at $2 each. Both directions, or it is not your position — a gross figure of skins won reads like a win when you are down.

Settled only, same rule as Nassau. A provisional pot has not paid anyone, so it is not in there.

Pool mode inverts it

Per-skin pays a fixed amount per hole won. Pool divides one pot among however many skins get won, which means every new skin makes yours worth less. The card says that out loud: $15 a skin, falling.

Nothing else about the composition changes. The pot slot holds the pool, the carry line holds the count so far.

Par is in the header

HOLE 12 · PAR 4. Skins is the one game where par belongs on the lock screen — net skins turn on strokes, and whether a 4 is good enough is the question the number is asking.

Always-on and the eight hours

One 36px number holds up under the brightness pull better than anything else drawn for this system. Skins is the easiest of the three to read at arm’s length in sun.

Starts on the first score posted, ends on round sign with the final state, dismisses after ~5 minutes.
