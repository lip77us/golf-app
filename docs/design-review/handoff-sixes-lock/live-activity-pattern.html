Live Activities — the pattern

Sixes is the worked example. This is what generalises out of it: five slots, one test for which games earn an activity at all, and a second test for which of them earn a push. The layout never changes between games — only what fills the slots does.

  • 4The sidesHEADLINE · MIDDLEWho is in it. Present only when it is not obvious or it changes — essential in Sixes, pointless in stroke play. This is the slot most games leave empty.
  • 5The stateHEADLINE · RIGHTThe match in a word — dormie, closed, all square — in the leading side’s colour. When no word applies it holds holes remaining. This is the slot that changes how the next hole is played, which is why it beat a running balance for the position.
  • 6The shapeFOOTERHow much game is left, with the stake and your running money beside it. A slot, not a component — three pips in Sixes, three in a Nassau, a carry count in skins, a thru number in stroke play. Whatever answers “how far in are we” in one glance.

Almost everything passes, which is the point worth noticing: the app is built on games where other people's scores move your money. The one that fails is the one with no bet on it.

Which of them earn a push

⚠A Live Activity that asks for something stops being a glance. The activity is ambient and always current. When a moment genuinely needs announcing, that is a notification — a different object, punctual, dismissed once read, with the activity underneath already showing the answer.
Earns a push
SixesThe hole-7 draw, and any extra-holes draw. Nothing else.
NassauA press against you. It changes what you owe, without your agreement.
SurvivorYour own elimination. Everyone else’s is not your business.
Ball gameYour group loses the ball. The moment the game ends for you.
CupThe cup is clinched. Once, and it is the whole event.
Does not
Every holeEighteen pushes is uninstall behaviour.
Position changesThe activity already shows it. That is what it is for.
Skins carryingCommon, expected, and visible on the pip. Only a settled carry of real size is arguable, and it is not built.
Someone else’s birdieNot news. Chat exists.
Round completeThe final state does this, quietly, on the activity that is already there.

Rules that hold across all of them

One per roundA foursome running Sixes and skins gets one activity. The game with the strongest news wins it — and the ranking is fixed at setup, not fought over at runtime. Two activities for one round is the worst outcome.
Neutral boardThe match belongs to the group and two men should be able to read one phone. Nothing personal sits in the headline band — money lives in the footer, beside the stake, where the context makes a label unnecessary.
Read-onlyNo buttons. Every action in these games is group state that wants the app’s confirmation, and a mis-tap on a lock screen is expensive.
Starts on the first scoreNot the tee time. Buys back the half hour on the range against the eight-hour limit, and an abandoned round leaves no ghost.
Ends personalWhatever the round was, the last state is what you won and who to see, held a few minutes, then dismissed.
Survives one nitIf a line needs a second glance on an always-on display, it does not belong in the activity. Useful test to apply before adding anything.

Open

Ranking the games“Strongest news wins the activity” needs an actual order when a foursome runs three games. Proposed: Cup > Sixes > Nassau > Survivor > skins > everything else — unconfirmed.
Tournament spectatorsA golfer not playing who wants the Cup on his lock screen. Plausible and not designed — it breaks the “one per round” rule, since he has no round.
Eight hours in a CupTwo-day events exceed the limit by a wide margin. The activity presumably restarts per round, but the handoff between rounds is undrawn.
WatchEvery one of these is a better watch complication than a lock-screen panel. Out of scope here and worth its own pass.
