Drive requirements

This is where a team round gets complicated. The scoring is straightforward either way; the drives are the part groups argue about, forget on the 15th tee, and work out wrong in the car park.

It applies to both formats — a shamble chooses a tee shot exactly as a scramble does. Four rules cover what clubs actually play, and they are not four settings of one thing: three are quotas and one is a schedule. That split decides the whole design.

NoneNo requirement. Take the best drive every time, and this screen never appears again.
Per nineEach golfer’s drive used at least once (or twice) on each nine. Two windows, and the front does not carry to the back.
Per eighteenTwo or three each across the round. One window, most slack.
Alternating pairsTwo men drive each hole, the other two the next. The team picks the two pairs on the 1st tee — not a quota, a rota.

Quotas need slack shown, schedules need the next hole shown

A quota is satisfied whenever you like, so the useful number is how much room is left: twelve drives required across eighteen holes means six free. That figure is what tells a captain whether he can let his long hitter drive the par 5.

A schedule has no slack at all. What it needs is one line on the tee: Gunst and Yau are up. No counting, no tracking, no warning — and nothing to fall short of, so the penalty setting does not apply to it.

The pairs are the team’s, set on the 1st tee

The app does not derive them from handicap. Four men standing on the first tee decide who is with whom — it takes ten seconds, it is the only part of the rule anyone enjoys, and a computed pairing would be overridden on the spot anyway.

So the rule has one setup moment inside the round: a card on hole 1, before the first score, asking for the split. After that it is fixed for eighteen holes and the app just names the pair on each tee. Fixed matters — a rota that can be re-cut mid-round is not a rota.

The warning has to come before it is too late, not on 18

The failure every group has had: three holes left, two men each owing two drives. Five drives, three holes. It became impossible two holes ago and nobody noticed.

So the tracker computes drives owed against holes remaining in the window and turns amber the moment the two cross. On the per-nine rule it does this twice, because a man short on the front is already short — the back nine cannot fix it, and a tracker that only totals eighteen will say he is fine until the 18th green.

Short teams and the phantom 4th

Three men at two each is six drives, not eight — but a three-man team is not really three. It fields a phantom 4th, so the quota is four men’s worth and the phantom’s share rotates: the man not driving takes the 1st and 4th shots, and who that is moves hole by hole.

Alternating pairs works with three

AB, then BC, then AC, repeat. Every hole has two drivers and every man sits out every third hole, which is as even as three into two goes. The man sitting out is the one covering the phantom — he takes the 1st and 4th shots.

In the quota rules the three rotate on a shot basis instead: whoever is not driving that hole plays the phantom’s ball. Either way the team hits four balls and the format is unchanged, which is the whole point of the phantom.

Falling short is the TD’s call, and it defaults to nothing

Most groups treat the requirement as honour and a shortfall as an embarrassment. The app warns and records, and by default does not punish — a two-stroke penalty per missing drive is available for TDs who run it that way, and it is off unless chosen. Silently disqualifying a team over a drive count would be the worst outcome the app could produce.

Where it lives

Its own step, not a toggle buried in the format screen. It changes the scorecard for both formats, so it is worth a screen; and a TD running no requirement passes it in one tap.
