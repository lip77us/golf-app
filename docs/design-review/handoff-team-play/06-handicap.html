Handicap on a team score

This is the step that decides whether anyone believes the result. A scramble handicap is not four handicaps — it is one number, built from four, and the maths is unfamiliar enough that a TD who cannot see it worked will not trust it.

The allowance is not a preference, it is a table

Each format has an established allowance. Scramble takes 25 / 20 / 15 / 10 of each man’s course handicap, lowest first, summed. Shamble applies a single percentage to each golfer’s own strokes — the fewer balls that count, the lower the percentage.

So the screen does not ask “what allowance”. It states the allowance the chosen format uses, shows it applied, and lets the TD override the whole thing with one number if his group has its own tradition. Presenting a table as an open question invites a guess.

Scramble25/20/15/10 → one team figure. Three men: a phantom 4th at the average of the three, then the same table.
Shamble85% of each golfer’s own course handicap at best 2 of 4. 75% at best 1, 95% at best 3.
OverrideOne percentage, applied flat. The screen keeps showing the worked result so the TD sees what he did.

Worked on his own teams, not on an example

The card shows Team 1’s four real men, their four percentages and the sum. A generic illustration proves nothing; his own numbers are the only ones he will check. Every team is listed with its figure so an outlier is visible here rather than at the scoring table.

The team handicap is a whole number

6.15 becomes 6. Golfers do not play fractional strokes and a card that says 6.15 looks like a spreadsheet error at the scoring table — whatever the arithmetic says, the number a team is given has to be one it can subtract in its head.

Round once, at the end. Rounding each man’s contribution first turns 1.00 + 1.60 + 1.65 + 1.90 into 1 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 7. Rounding the sum gives 6. A full stroke of difference from where you put the rounding, so the screen shows the raw total next to the rounded one and the code computes at full precision throughout.

Half rounds up: Clay’s 7.50 becomes 8.

Which means ties are real again, and they split

Whole strokes across six teams will produce a tie most weeks. There is no countback and no card-off — tied teams combine the places they occupy and split the money, the same rule the Individual flow uses. A tie is no action, and an arbitrary tiebreaker decides real money on a rule nobody agreed to at the first tee.

A three-man team is handicapped as four

Dune fields a phantom 4th at the average of its three men — 9, 15 and 23 give 16 — and then takes the ordinary table. No special three-man percentages, no separate code path, and the phantom sorts into the order like anyone else.

The team lands on 10, two more than a three-man allowance would have given it. That is the right answer: it is playing four balls, so it is handicapped for four.

Lowest first, and it is shown that way

The order is the rule — 25% attaches to the lowest handicap, not to the captain or the first name on the list. Sorting the four men low to high on screen makes the order self-evident and stops a TD reading the percentages as arbitrary.

Course handicap, not index

Allowance applies to course handicap, after the tee adjustment. The tees are already set; the screen names the tee it computed from so nobody applies 25% to the wrong number.
