How many balls count, and where

A shamble is only a format once you say how many of the four scores count on a hole. One number for all eighteen is the simple case; most groups that run a shamble seriously do something more interesting than that.

It expands in place, under the radio

House rule from the Irish Rumble work: no game gets a second rules screen. Picking Shamble reveals the count controls beneath it, and picking Scramble collapses them. There is no extra wizard step and no back-and-forth to check what you set.

Four modes, and only one of them is fiddly

FixedThe same count all eighteen. Best 2 of 4 is the default and the answer for most rounds.
EscalatingOne ball on 1–6, two on 7–12, three on 13–18. The round gets harder as it goes and the back nine needs everyone.
Par-basedPar 3 counts three balls, par 4 counts two, par 5 counts one. The short holes are where a team can afford to need everybody.
Per-holeThe 18-cell grid. Tap to cycle 1→2→3→4.

Escalating is drawn as a preset rather than left to the grid because it is the shape people describe in words — sixes — and making them tap eighteen cells to express it is the app failing to listen.

The preview reads it back in sentences

Whatever the mode, the same block underneath says which holes count how many, collapsed into runs. It is the check that catches a per-hole grid that is accidentally all 2s, or a par-based setting on a course with five par 3s that turns out much harder than intended.

Total balls is the number that describes the round

36 counted balls of 72 played tells a TD more than any of the settings do. Fixed at 2 gives 36. Escalating gives 36 as well — the same total, distributed differently — and seeing that is what makes the choice feel like a choice about character rather than difficulty.

Four balls on a hole means everybody counts

Legal, and worth a word: a 4-ball hole has no drop score, so one blow-up is the team’s. It is a real design choice for a closing hole and a bad accident anywhere else, so the grid says so when a 4 is set.

A three-man team counts the phantom

Dune plays three men and a phantom 4th. On a hole counting three balls, the phantom’s ball is one of the four available — the same as any other team. Nothing about the count changes for a short team; that is the point of handicapping it as four.

Locked with the format

The counts lock when the first score lands, alongside the format itself. A hole already scored under “best 2” cannot be re-read as “best 1”.
