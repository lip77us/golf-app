Settlement — one pool

Individual settlement reconciles seven pots against sixteen golfers. This reconciles one pot against twenty-three, and the screen should look as different as the problem is. No tabs, no per-game sections, no balance check across games — one number in, three payments out.

Everyone paid the same, so the screen leads with what changed

Flat $25 at signup, no optional games, so the entry side has nothing to explain. One line for it. The whole screen is the three prizes and who is in them.

The team wins; the golfers get paid

Prizes are earned by teams and settled to people — the TD hands cash to men, not to a colour. So every paid place expands to its members with each man’s figure spelled out. A row saying Fern — $287.50 is unactionable at the scoring table.

A tie is the normal case now, not an exception

Whole-stroke handicaps across six teams tie most weeks. This week there are two, and they behave differently — which is the case worth drawing.

The rule: combine the places the tied teams occupy, and split what those places pay. Slate and Dune tied on 57, so 2nd and 3rd combine to $287.50 and each team takes $143.75. Pine and Clay tied on 58 for 4th; 4th and 5th pay nothing, so the tie costs nothing and is left unresolved. No countback, no card-off, matching the Individual flow.

Each tie is drawn as one block with both teams inside it. Two separate rows each reading $143.75 would hide that it was one prize.

The penny rule, stated on the screen

$287.50 over four men is $71.875. Splitting to the cent leaves two cents unassigned, and a settlement that does not add up is worse than one that is slightly arbitrary.

The rule: split down to the cent, odd cents to the team’s highest course handicap. Deterministic, always balances, and it is written under the split rather than left as an unexplained $71.89 next to three $71.87s.

A three-man team splits three ways and takes more each

Dune collects $143.75 over three men rather than four — $47.91 each against Slate’s $35.93 for the identical placing. The phantom 4th earned the strokes and cannot be paid.

That is the same rule the Irish Rumble uses for a levelled group: the place belongs to the team, and it is divided among the men who are actually on it. It is consistent, so it stays — and the screen says 3 ways on the row so nobody has to work out why the figure is larger.

PaysThree places, 50 / 30 / 20. Six teams — half the field cashes, which is what a one-day scramble wants. A tie can collapse three places into two payments.
TiesCombined and split. Expected, now that the handicap is a whole number.
Drive shortfallRecorded on the team row if it happened. It changes the money only if the TD chose the stroke penalty, and then it has already changed the gross upstream.

Settle is gated on all six teams signed

Same rule as everywhere else: money does not move while a score can. Until then every figure is muted italic and the button says what it is waiting on.

The receipt is the same one

Texting works exactly as it does for Individual play — personal receipts or a field summary — and needs nothing new here. A team receipt is a short one: one entry line, one prize line if he cashed.
