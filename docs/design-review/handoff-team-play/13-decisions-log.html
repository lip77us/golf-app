Team Play — decisions

Sixty-six calls made while drawing the four-man team flow. Amber rows are still open. The rule throughout: this is the simplest of the three tournament shapes and should stay that way — most entries here are a decision not to build something.

Shape and scopeWizard step 1 →

QuestionCallWhy
What is the tournamentMany 4-man teams, one leaderboardThe third shape. Cup is two sides and a match; Individual is a field of singles; this is small teams against each other.
How many roundsOne. Stated, not chosenA club wanting two days runs two tournaments. Carrying best-N counting and round columns into a Saturday scramble costs three screens and buys nothing.
Team count or field firstField first, app dividesAsking for a team count makes the TD do division before he has a headcount.
Side gamesNoneThe team is the foursome, so there is no field to run a ball game against. A foursome wanting a bet among its own men still has the casual games.
MoneyOne pool, team championshipFlat per-golfer entry at signup. Nothing optional, so the entry side of settlement has nothing to explain. The fee, places and split are set on step 6.
Course and teesThe existing Set tees screen, step 2Every golfer needs a tee before a course handicap exists, and the allowance is a percentage of course handicap. Nothing new was built — a team round needs tees for exactly the reason a casual round does.
How teams get builtTD assigns manuallyNo draft, no auto-balance, no random draw. He already knows who wants to play with whom.

FormatTeam format →

QuestionCallWhy
Which formatsScramble and ShambleTwo formats, two scorecards. They share one thing — the tee shot is chosen — which is why drives apply to both.
Irish Rumble as a team formatRemovedIt is a shamble with the count escalating. A club that wants it has it casually and as an Individual side game; a third tournament format would add a screen to say what the count chip already says.
A “modified — pick the count” optionNot offeredSame reason. The shamble count chip is the dial.
Shamble countFixed, escalating, par-based or per holeBest 2 of 4 is the default. Escalating — 1 ball on 1–6, 2 on 7–12, 3 on 13–18 — is a preset because it is the shape people describe in words. Making them tap eighteen cells for it is the app failing to listen.
Where the count controls liveUnder the radio, in placeHouse rule from the Irish Rumble work: no game gets a second rules screen.
PreviewRuns, in sentences, plus a totalHoles 1–6, best 1 net. And 36 counted of 72 played — the number that describes the round. Fixed-at-2 and escalating both give 36, distributed differently, which makes the choice read as character rather than difficulty.
A hole counting all fourAllowed, flaggedNo drop score — one blow-up is the team’s. Deliberate on a closing hole, an accident anywhere else.
Short team and the countNo changeThe phantom’s ball is one of the four available. That is the point of handicapping a three-man team as four.
Format changeable mid-roundLocked at first scoreA one-number card cannot be re-read as four.
Can teams have 3 menYes, one short team23 golfers is the normal case, not the edge case.
Borrow a 4thNever offeredA borrowed golfer would hit shots for a team he is competing against — every good one costs his own team the pot. No version of that is defensible.
Short team insteadA phantom 4thA fourth slot handicapped at the average of the three real men. Four handicaps, so the ordinary 25/20/15/10 applies with no special table; four balls, so the format is unchanged.
Who plays the phantom’s ballThe man not drivingHe takes the 1st and 4th shots, and who that is rotates hole by hole.
Phantom handicapAverage of the three, roundedBellini 9, Kwan 15, Ortega 23 → 16. Dune plays off 10.
Phantom vs a three-man allowanceTwo strokes more generous, and correct30/20/10 would give 8, and 25/20/15 on three would give 9. Both take strokes away from a team that is already short a ball.
Alternative for the short teamPlay short — same 10, three ballsThe allowance follows the roster, not the number of balls hit. Dropping the fourth row of the table would give 9 — fewer strokes for fewer balls, which is backwards.

DrivesDrive requirement →

QuestionCallWhy
Is it worth a stepYes, its own screenThis is where a team round actually gets complicated. Four rules, a quota that can silently become impossible, and a tap added to every hole.
Which rulesNone, per nine, per eighteen, alternating pairsWhat clubs actually play. Three are quotas; one is a schedule.
Quota vs scheduleDifferent UI for eachA quota needs slack shown — twelve required of eighteen, six free. A schedule needs one line on the tee: Gunst and Yau are up.
Per-nine windowsTwo, independentThe front does not carry to the back. A tracker that only totals eighteen says a man is fine until the 18th green.
When to warnThe moment owed > holes left in the windowThe failure every group has had: three holes left, five drives owed. It became impossible two holes ago and nobody noticed.
Warn on the card tooYes, on the drive row every hole“Taking Detomasi’s drive here leaves the front nine short.” The consequence in a sentence, on the tee.
Block the tapNeverThe team may knowingly take the shortfall.
Penalty for falling shortNone by defaultMost groups treat it as honour. Two strokes per missing drive is available and off unless chosen. Silently disqualifying a team over a drive count would be the worst thing the app could do.
Who sets the alternating pairsThe team, on the 1st teeFour men decide who is with whom in ten seconds. A computed pairing would be overridden on the spot. Fixed for eighteen holes once set — a rota that can be re-cut mid-round is not a rota.
Alternating pairs with three menAB, BC, AC, repeatTwo drivers every hole; each man sits out every third hole, which is as even as three into two goes. The man sitting out plays the phantom’s ball.
Phantom’s ball in the quota rulesRotates on a shot basisWhoever is not driving that hole takes the 1st and 4th shots. The team hits four balls either way, which is the whole point of the phantom.
Penalty on a schedule ruleDoes not applyThere is nothing to fall short of.
Short team quotaFour men’s worthThe team fields a phantom, so it owes four drives, not three — the phantom’s share rotates through the three real men.
Which formatsBothA shamble chooses a tee shot exactly as a scramble does, so the requirement, the tracker and the warning are identical.

HandicapHandicap & allowance →

QuestionCallWhy
Allowance modelPercentage, set by formatEach format has an established table. The screen states it rather than asking — presenting a table as an open question invites a guess.
Scramble allowance25 / 20 / 15 / 10, summedOne team figure built from four handicaps, lowest first.
Shamble allowanceTracks the average ball count85% at two balls a hole, 75% at one, 95% at three. A per-hole grid averaging 2.3 gets 95% suggested rather than 85%.
OverrideOne flat percentageA group’s tradition beats the table. The screen keeps showing the worked result so the TD sees what he did.
Show the mathsWorked on his own teamA generic illustration proves nothing. His own four men and their four percentages are the only numbers he will check.
Member orderSorted low to high, alwaysThe percentage is positional, so a manual order would be a lie.
Round the team handicapYes — whole strokesGolfers do not play fractional strokes, and 6.15 on a card looks like a spreadsheet error at the scoring table.
Where the rounding happensOnce, on the totalRounding each contribution first turns 1.00+1.60+1.65+1.90 into 7. Rounding the sum gives 6. Compute at full precision, round at the end, show the raw total underneath.
Half strokesRound upClay’s 7.50 becomes 8.
Index or course handicapCourse, tee named on screenSo nobody applies 25% to the wrong number.

QuestionCallWhy
Show balance while buildingYes — allowance per team, plus a spread stripManual does not mean blind. A hand-built scramble is usually unbalanced by accident and only discovered on the leaderboard.
Show each man’s contributionYes, next to his nameMaiolini 4 → 1.00. Showing only the team total hides why moving one man swings a team by two strokes.
What Next waits onAn empty trayBalance is advice; a golfer with no team is a broken tournament. And the button says which it is waiting on.
Team namesColour default, free text over it, 16 charsSame cap as the ball game. Colour is assigned regardless because it does real work on the board and the card.
Empty seatsNamed, not blank2 seats open. An invisible gap is how a golfer gets left off.

Play and moneyLeaderboard →

QuestionCallWhy
Scramble card shapeA stepper, one huge numberFour boxes with three ignored is the easiest way to get a scramble card wrong. It is tapped by a man on the next tee holding a beer.
Scramble default valueParA scramble team makes par more than anything else, and a wrong default beats no default when the alternative is a keypad.
Shamble cardFour scores, counting ones tinted liveTwo men’s cards do nothing on a given hole. A man who shot 5 must see instantly that it was not used, or the total looks wrong and someone re-enters it.
Show the count per holeYes, in the headerIt does not change across the eighteen, and saying so costs one line and settles the recurring question at the green.
Net on the cardNoA whole-number team figure applied to the round is not a stroke on a hole. Showing 6 invites subtracting it per hole.
Who entersAnyone on the teamSix teams entering their own hole keeps the board live. One designated scorer means the board is dark until 18.
Leaderboard sortNet, ascending, one columnOne round, one game, six rows. The design problem is resisting additions.
Show the phantomAs a row, everywhereItalic and undraggable on the team card, named on the leaderboard row, listed unpaid in settlement. It is a player as far as every screen after setup is concerned; hiding it makes all of them look like special cases.
TiebreakNone — combine and splitWhole strokes across six teams tie most weeks. Tied teams combine the places they occupy and split what those places pay. No countback, matching the Individual flow — an arbitrary tiebreaker decides real money on a rule nobody agreed to at the first tee.
Teams still outMarked, not sorted awayA team leading through fourteen is not leading. Same rule as the Individual board.
Places paidTD’s choice — drawn at three, 50/30/20Six teams, so half the field cashes. That is what a one-day scramble wants, and it is the default rather than the rule.
Prizes shown per manYes, every place expandedThe team wins; the TD hands cash to people. Fern — $287.50 is unactionable at the scoring table.
Odd centsTo the team’s highest course handicapNeither $287.50 nor a split $57.50 divides by four. Arbitrary but stated and deterministic — a settlement that does not balance is worse.
Three-man team’s shareSplit three ways, more eachDune takes $47.91 a man against Slate’s $35.93 for the same placing. The phantom earned the strokes and cannot be paid — the same rule as the Irish Rumble levelled group.
Receipt and textingReused unchangedA team receipt is simply a short one — one entry line, one prize line if he cashed.

Entry and payoutEntry & payout →

QuestionCallWhy
Are fee, places and split TD-settableYes, all three, one screenThey are the only numbers anybody argues about after the round, and changing any one changes the other two’s meaning.
Where in the flowStep 6, after teamsIt needs the field size to compute the pot and the team count to cap the places. Neither is known until the teams are built.
Show percentages or dollarsBoth, liveA TD setting 50/30/20 is choosing what third place gets, not choosing percentages. The percentage is the mechanism.
Per-man figureUnder every place$287.50 to a team means nothing until it is divided. A man decides by whether $25 is worth maybe winning $72.
Fee steps$5Nobody charges $23.
How many places1–4, capped at the team countSix teams paying five places is a raffle. Half the field cashing is advice on the screen, not a rule — winner-takes-all is a legitimate preset.
Split must total 100Enforced, with the reason on the button95% leaves money in the TD’s pocket with no line explaining it. The shortfall is named in dollars.
RoundingNot the TD’s problemHe works in whole percentages. The app handles cents and settlement states where the odd ones went.
A place paying less than entryAllowed, flaggedA team can finish in the money and still be down. Worth knowing, not worth blocking — unlike the Individual day bet, nothing here is disqualified by cashing.
Ties on the payout screenWarned about hereThree places is three prizes, not three teams paid. Cheaper to read at setup than to discover at the scoring table.
