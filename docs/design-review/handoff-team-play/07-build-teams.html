Building the teams

The TD assigns every golfer himself. No draft, no auto-balance, no random draw — he already knows who wants to play with whom, and the app fighting that is worse than the app doing nothing.

Manual, but not blind

Doing it by hand does not mean doing it without information. Each team shows its allowance figure the moment a fourth man lands in it, and the strip under the header shows the six figures against each other. The TD is not being told what to do; he is being told what he just did.

That is the whole argument for this screen. A hand-built scramble is usually unbalanced by accident — four low men end up together because they arrived together — and it is only discovered on the leaderboard.

The percentage sits next to the man

Each member row carries the strokes he contributes: Maiolini 4 → 1.00. Move him to another team and the number changes, because the percentage is positional. Showing only the team total hides why moving one man swings a team by two strokes.

The contributions stay fractional and the team figure is the rounded one, with the raw sum under it. Those are the two numbers a TD needs: the one he plays with, and the one that explains it.

OrderMembers sort low to high automatically. The percentage follows the position, so a manual order would be a lie.
UnassignedA tray at the bottom, always visible, with a count. Nobody gets left off because he scrolled out of view.
Empty seatNamed — 1 seat open — not an invisible gap.

The short team is a decision, not a leftover

23 golfers means one team of three. The screen does not choose which — the TD does — but the three-man team carries a phantom 4th row, italic and undraggable, handicapped at the average of the three. It sorts into the order like anyone else and takes its percentage like anyone else, so the team’s figure comes from the ordinary table.

Drawing it as a row rather than a footnote is the point: the phantom is a player as far as every screen after this one is concerned. It appears on the card, in the drive rota and in the allowance, and hiding it here would make all three look like special cases.

Names are the TD’s, colours are for the card

Teams default to a colour name — Pine, Clay, Slate — and are free text over it. The colour is doing real work on the leaderboard and the scorecard, so it is assigned whether or not the TD renames anything. Sixteen characters, same cap as the ball game.

What Next is waiting on

Every golfer assigned. Not a balanced field, not a full six teams — just nobody in the tray. Balance is advice; a man with no team is a broken tournament. And the button says which it is waiting on.
