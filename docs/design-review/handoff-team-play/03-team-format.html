How a team makes a score

Two formats, and the choice reaches further than any other answer in the wizard. It sets how many numbers get entered per hole and what the handicap even is.

ScrambleAll four hit, best ball is played, repeat. One score per hole. Handicap is a single team number.
ShambleBest drive, then each man plays his own ball in. Four scores per hole, the best two count. Handicap stays per golfer.

Two formats, two scorecards, and they share exactly one thing: the tee shot is chosen. That is why the drive requirement applies to both.

The ball count expands in place

Picking Shamble reveals the count controls under the radio — fixed, escalating by sixes, par-based, or the 18-cell grid. House rule from the Irish Rumble work: no game gets a second rules screen. That state is drawn separately for detail; in the product it is this screen with Shamble selected.

Two, not five

Irish Rumble, modified counts, best-ball variants — all of them are a shamble with the count moved. They are not offered. A club that wants Irish Rumble has it as a casual game and as an Individual side game; making it a third tournament format would add a screen to say something the shamble count chip already says.

A short team is a handicap problem, not a staffing problem

Borrowing does not work in a team championship. A borrowed golfer would be hitting shots for a team he is competing against, and every shot he hits well costs his own team the pot. There is no version of that a TD can defend at the scoring table.

So nobody is borrowed — the option does not exist. A three-man team fields a phantom 4th instead: a fourth slot on the card whose handicap is the average of the three real men, and whose ball is played by the team itself.

That keeps everything downstream identical. Four handicaps, so the ordinary 25/20/15/10 applies with no special table. Four balls on a scramble hole, so the format is unchanged. The only thing that differs is who hits the phantom’s shots, and that is a drive-rule question, answered on the next step.

Fewer balls must never mean fewer strokes

The obvious way to handle a short team is to drop the bottom row of the table and give the three men 25/20/15. That produces 9 for Dune, against 10 with a phantom.

Which is backwards. A team playing three balls is at a disadvantage to one playing four, and the mechanical answer takes a stroke away from it for exactly that reason. The rule that avoids it: the allowance follows the roster, not the number of balls hit. Three men get a fourth handicap either way — the phantom’s — and the only thing “play short” changes is whether anybody hits its ball.

So the two options carry the same number, and the difference is stated in words rather than smuggled into the arithmetic.

Drive requirements are their own step, and apply to both

Both formats choose a tee shot, so both can require that everyone’s gets used. It is the part of a team round that actually gets complicated — four different rules, a quota that can quietly become impossible, and one tap added to every hole. Too much to hang off a toggle here.

Format is locked once a score lands

Changing scramble to shamble mid-round would invalidate every hole already entered — a one-number card cannot be re-read as four. The screen says so before it matters, not after.
