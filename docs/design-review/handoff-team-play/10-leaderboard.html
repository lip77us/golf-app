Six teams, one column

The Individual board has to carry four rounds, five games and a counting rule. This one carries one round, one game, six rows. The design problem is entirely different: not how to fit everything, but how to resist adding anything.

It is the same board as everywhere else

The card, the rank / name / gross / net columns, the hole-grid strip under an expanded row, the circled birdie and boxed bogey — all of it is the leaderboard the casual rounds and the Cup already use. A team is just what sits in the name column.

Nothing here earned a bespoke board. Inventing one would mean a golfer who reads three leaderboards a month has to learn a fourth layout to find out he finished fourth.

Net is a whole number, and ties happen

The allowance is rounded once at the end, so the board reads 55, not 54.90. Nobody plays a fractional stroke and nobody wants to read one on a leaderboard.

The cost is real: whole strokes across six teams produce ties most weeks, and here there are two. Slate and Dune share 57; Pine and Clay share 58. They are shown as T2 and T4, in board order, with no attempt to separate them — tied teams combine the places they occupy and split the money. No countback, no card-off, the same rule as the Individual flow.

The two ties behave differently and that is worth reading twice. T2 combines 2nd and 3rd, so those two teams split $287.50. T4 combines 4th and 5th, neither of which pays, so it costs nothing to leave unresolved.

Ranked onNet, ascending. One sort, no segmented control to change it.
TiesMarked T, drawn adjacent, split in settlement. Never silently ordered.
GrossThe middle column. It is what the team remembers shooting and it is not what pays.

The phantom is named on the row

Dune reads Bellini · Kwan · Ortega · phantom 4th. Three names and ten strokes against four names and eight would look like a mistake; naming the phantom explains the figure in the space already there.

Teams still out are marked, not sorted away

An amber dot and thru 14. A team leading through fourteen is not leading, and the board must not let that read as a result — the same rule as the Individual championship board.

Colour does the identifying

Six team names, most of them one syllable, all unfamiliar. The colour block carries the row and matches the scorecard the team is holding, so a man finds his team without reading. That is also why colour is assigned even when the TD renames the team.

The pool sits under the board, not on it

One card: $575, three places, projected until every team is in. Money on the rows would put a dollar figure next to a team that has four holes left.

No tabs, or one

With no side games there is nothing to tab between. If a TD later turns one on, the tab bar fills with the game named — it does not sit there empty waiting to be useful.
