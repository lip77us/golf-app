Teams

Teams need names, and this is the step that collects them — step 3, after the event exists and before anybody is drafted into it. The shipped screen sets a team count and a team colour but never a name, which is why every downstream screen says “Team 1 / Team 2”.

Why 16 characters

The cap is set by the narrowest place a team name has to render on one line — the leaderboard score header on an iPhone 13 mini, the smallest screen we support.

Cap16 chars
  • 16 fits every single-line surface: board header, match card, draft roster.
  • Over 12 wraps to two lines in the compact hub tally, which is why that row was given a wrapping name and a smaller score.
  • The counter appears from 12 characters on, not from the first keystroke — a limit you are nowhere near is noise.

Other fixes on this step

  • Colours are swatches, not tinted word-pills. The shipped control renders the colour name in that colour with a pale fill, so unselected options read as disabled. A filled square with a check is unambiguous.
  • A colour taken by another team is struck through and locked. Two teams could previously pick the same colour, which makes the whole red/blue system meaningless.
  • 2 teams is genuinely selected on arrival and Next is enabled — the shipped screen highlights 2 but keeps Next disabled until a chip is tapped.
  • The cup name is not asked here. It belongs to event details at step 2, and asking twice is how the shipped flow ended up with three owners for the player list. The name is whatever the captain typed — nothing is appended, so a cup called Tilden Ryder Cup was typed in full.
  • Names are optional with a real default: leave a field alone and it stays Team 1 / Team 2, so the step never blocks a captain in a hurry.

The badge is editable, and collisions are allowed

We do not block two teams from sharing a first letter. “Berkeley Blues” and “Bucko’s Bandits’” are both legitimate, and rejecting a name for a reason the user cannot see is the worst kind of validation.

  • The badge is its own field, one or two characters, sitting beside the name. It auto-derives (leading “Team ” stripped, so Team Bucko gives B) and keeps following the name until you type in it — after that it is yours and stops changing.
  • A duplicate badge warns, it does not block. Colour is the primary identifier and it is already unique by construction; the badge is a secondary cue for greyscale and print, so a clash is a nuisance rather than an error.
  • Two characters exist for the case that solves most clashes — BB and BL — and the chip is sized to hold them at every size it appears in.
