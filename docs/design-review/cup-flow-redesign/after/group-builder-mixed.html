Group builder — mixed cup

The Triple Cup builder can open with the golfer list, because every group plays the same thing. A mixed cup cannot: until you know the group’s game, you do not know how many golfers it holds or which sides they come from.

So the day’s plan is the worklist. Every match step 5 paid for is listed as a thing to build, and the captain picks which one to build next — the app never decides the order.

The game decides the shape

  • Nassau pairs, Chapman, Fourball → a foursome, 2 per side. The builder shows two slots a side and refuses to save a 3–1.
  • Singles, Singles Nassau → a twosome, 1 per side.
  • Irish Rumble, Scramble → four from one side. It is one entry on the list that builds two groups, because half a match is not a thing a captain can save.

The plan is the worklist

  • Step 5 said how many of each game Saturday runs, so every match is known before anyone is placed. The list shows them all: built ones with their group and tee time, unbuilt ones as work.
  • Order is the captain’s. Build the Rumble first because those eight are standing there, or leave the singles for last. Group numbers are assigned by tee time on save, so working out of order costs nothing.
  • The counter strip is the same arithmetic as the step-5 meter, read from the other end: there it was golfers to place, here it is matches left to build. A game with nothing left reads built.

Carried over from the Triple Cup builder

  • Tee time defaults to the previous group plus the interval, and the interval is remembered per course.
  • Shotgun is available here and it was not for Triple Cup — a mixed day has no shared segment split to protect, so groups can start on different holes.

Allowance is set on the match

  • Every game arrives with its allowance already filled in — the USGA number for that format. A captain who wants the standard does nothing; the card states what will be applied and moves on.
  • It sits on the match, not the cup. A Chapman group and a Singles group in the same round take different allowances, and the only screen that knows which game a group is playing is this one.
  • Two shapes, following the Triple Cup Foursomes control: a percentage per golfer where each golfer plays a ball, and a combined side handicap — weights on the low and high index — where the side plays one.
  • The card shows the result, not just the setting. Course handicap, the percentage, and the strokes it produces — so a wrong number is visible before the round starts rather than argued about on the 4th tee.
  • Changed from standard, the badge says so. There is nothing to hunt for and nothing hidden behind an advanced screen.

Still open

  • A Rumble is one row that builds two groups. Drawn here as a single entry that opens both halves in turn — whether the second half should be its own row is open.
  • Whether an edited allowance should offer “apply to every Chapman match this round”. Eight matches of the same game means eight identical edits.
