Tournament side game

Shipped step 18 is called Secondary game and offers Skins, Nassau and rabbit alongside Irish Rumble. Those are not the same kind of thing: the first three are settled inside a foursome, the last two are scored across the field. Mixing them here is why the step contradicts the exclusive-format rule two screens later.

Format on this round

What the split fixes

  • One scope per step. A tournament side game is played by every group and ranked across the field. A group bet is money between four people. They need different screens because they need different scoreboards.
  • The exclusive rule becomes legible. On Triple Cup the step is struck, and it says why: the segments own all 18 holes, so there is no room for a field-wide game beside them.
  • Group bets are not lost, they are relocated — and the struck state links to where. Being exclusive at tournament scope does not stop a foursome having a Nassau.
  • None is a first-class answer, and the default. Most cups do not run one.

Still open

  • Whether a field-wide game can differ per round. The data allows it; a cup that runs Pink Ball on Saturday only is plausible, but the round picker adds a dimension to a step most people answer once.
  • Irish Rumble needs its own setup (which holes, whose ball) — a sub-screen not drawn here.

Why: its three segments are defined by hole — Fourball 1–6, Foursomes 7–12, Singles 13–18 — so every hole is already committed to a match. A field-wide game would have to share holes with one of them.
