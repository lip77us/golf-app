Group builder

Building one group currently takes three screens — pick the players, set four tees, set a tee time — and you walk all three again for every group. This is one screen, repeated once per group, and it is the same screen for every tournament type.

What the merge buys

  • Tee time is proposed, not asked. It defaults to one tee interval after the previous group — the answer in almost every case — with ±5 to adjust. A clock dial for a value that is always previous + interval is the most work for the most predictable input.
  • The interval is a course property, not a constant. Seven, eight and nine minutes are all common; a hardcoded ten just moves the work into the stepper. It is set once, remembered for the course, and every later group follows it.
  • Shotgun is the same card, not another screen. Start format is a round setting, so the toggle carries a note saying so. In shotgun the time collapses to one value for the whole field and the per-group question becomes which hole — suggested by group number, with holes already assigned shown as taken.
  • Triple Cup is sequential only, by rule. Its segments are defined by hole — Fourball 1–6, Foursomes 7–12, Singles 13–18 — so a shotgun would hand each group a different split and change the balance of the cup. Rather than warn after the fact, the toggle shows shotgun as unavailable and says why. Stroke play, skins and singles rounds can use it.
  • Tees live next to the golfers, so a mixed group is visible while you build it, and Set all handles the common case of one tee for the group. The shipped dropdown covers the very row it belongs to.
  • Composition is checked as you go, and cannot be broken: once a side has its two, that side’s remaining golfers dim to Team full. Preventing 3 v 1 costs less than explaining it. The badge still names an over-filled side, for the case where a golfer’s team changes after the group was built.
  • All three legal shapes save. Screen 24 already allows 2 v 2, 1 v 2 with a phantom filling the solo side, and 1 v 1 as an 18-hole Nassau — so a four-seat requirement would reject a legal group. Below four, the remaining seats read Optional and the card states what the shape means rather than leaving the captain to remember.
  • The pool shows who is spoken for. Golfers already in Group 1 stay visible and dimmed with their group named, so “where did Webb go” is answerable without leaving.
  • Index is shown everywhere a golfer is picked. Add players shows it, Set matches does not — and the index is the reason you would pair two people.

Side games are not the TD’s business

An earlier pass put a group bet control on this card — skins, Nassau, rabbit, per group. Removed. A wager inside a foursome is between those four people, and nothing about it needs a tournament director’s hand: he is not in it, does not collect it, and cannot be asked to remember it while seating sixteen golfers.

  • The foursome starts its own. There is already a mechanism for a group to open a side game and attach it to their tournament entry — the same one multi-group skins will use. That screen is drawn. This screen should not duplicate it, and a TD-facing control would make the captain the owner of a decision that is not his.
  • What stays here is what only the TD can set: who is in the group, which tee each golfer plays, when they go out, and whether the field is sequential or shotgun. Those are field-management facts. A bet is not.
  • The wizard’s side-game step is a different thing and still belongs there — Irish Rumble or Pink Ball is played by every foursome and ranked across the field, so somebody has to declare it once for everyone. That is the test: if it needs one answer for the whole field, it is the TD’s; if it settles among four, it is theirs.
  • The cup match in a 1 v 1 group is still not called a Nassau on this screen, even though it is one in structure — that word belongs to the group’s own game.

Same screen, other tournament types

  • Stroke or Stableford: the team chips and the 2 v 2 rule drop out; the check becomes a simple count of 2–4. Everything else is identical, which is what makes this worth building once.
  • Odd fields: a group of three is legal for stroke play and needs a phantom for cup play — the check is the right place to say so.

Shotgun rules

  • One group to a hole, assigned on creation. A new group takes the lowest free hole, so a TD who adds four groups gets 1, 2, 3, 4 without touching the picker. Taken holes are locked in it rather than hidden, because the useful question on the picker is who is on 7, not what is left.
  • Above 18 groups it is a double shotgun — 1A and 1B share a hole and go out together. The picker offers the B slot only once every hole is taken, so the second wave cannot start before the first is full. Not drawn.
  • One time for the field. In shotgun the per-group tee time collapses to a single round value; the interval control is hidden rather than disabled, because there is no interval to set.
  • Formats that define segments by hole cannot shotgun. Triple Cup’s Fourball 1–6 / Foursomes 7–12 / Singles 13–18 would hand a group starting on 9 a different game from a group starting on 2, so the toggle is struck with that reason rather than left to fail at scoring.
  • The interval belongs to the course, not the round. A new field on the course record, in minutes, default 10. The builder writes it when a TD changes it and reads it as the default for every later round at that course — which is what makes it worth storing at all. Ten common values are offered; the field accepts any integer.

Still open

  • Shotgun breaks the segment-to-hole mapping — settled by ruling it out for Triple Cup rather than by making segments relative to the starting hole. If a future format wants both, the leaderboard’s “Starts 13” labels have to name each group’s real hole.
  • Double shotgun — more than 18 groups needs 1A / 1B on the same hole. The picker is the right place for it; not drawn here.
  • Whether to offer auto-fill — seed all groups by index and let the captain adjust. Faster for 16 players, but it hides the pairing decision, which is half the fun.
