Handicap

Shipped 16 is titled Round Details and does four jobs: switch course, set the date, choose a handicap mode, and toggle a net double-bogey cap. Three of those belong somewhere else, and one does not belong in a cup at all.

Course, dateMoved to event details at step 2, where rounds are created. Shipped 16 is the third screen to ask for a course.
Net %Kept, plus the combined foursomes allowance casual already has. Shipped 16 offers neither.
Double-bogey capStruck for match play, with the reason attached. It caps a stroke total; match play has no total to protect.
“SO Low”Spelled out: strokes off the low index, with what that does to a card.

Matching casual Triple Cup

Casual Triple Cup already answers this properly, and the tournament wizard should not invent a second model. Its handicap card carries three things, and all three are here:

ModeNet · Gross · SO Low
Net %One scale, 50–100%
FoursomesLow % + High %, combined
  • The alt-shot allowance is a side number, not a per-golfer percentage. One ball means one handicap for the pair, so the control takes 50% of the low + 50% of the high — the USGA default, and the shape casual already uses. A single Net % cannot express it, which is why it is its own card rather than a third row in a list.
  • Net % applies to the segments where each golfer plays a ball — fourball and singles. Foursomes is scaled by its own two numbers instead.
  • The SO Low explanation is the casual copy, unchanged. It is the one sentence that makes the mode legible, and rewording it for the tournament would be two explanations of one rule.
  • The worked example uses the four course handicaps from the casual setup — 11, 16, 19, 24 — so the two screens can be compared line for line.

Why the cap has no meaning here

Net double bogey exists to stop one disaster hole from wrecking a score — a stroke-play total, or a Stableford points tally. In match play a hole is won, lost or halved, so a nine on the fourth costs exactly one hole, the same as a bogey would. Capping it changes nothing, and offering the toggle implies it changes something.

It stays on the screen rather than disappearing, struck with its reason, for the same argument as the exclusive side game: a control that vanished looks like a missing feature.

Still open

  • Segment order and stake are the casual screen’s other two cards — the hole ranges with a Play Foursomes first toggle, and one cup payout. Neither is a handicap, so they belong on points by round at step 5. Not yet added there.
  • Whether strokes off low should be a mode at all, or a checkbox on Net. It is arithmetically net with a shifted baseline, and two modes that produce the same card for a scratch field may be one mode too many.
  • The worked example uses the lowest and highest index in the draft — but the draft happens at step 6, two steps after this one. Until then it has to use the roster, or say it is illustrative.
  • Whether the two-man and four-man families need a combined allowance control here, which is a different shape again: one number for a side rather than a percentage per golfer.

Handicap

How strokes are given across the cup. Applies to every round.

Mode
NetEvery golfer plays off their own course handicap.
GrossNo strokes given. The card is the card.
Strokes off the lowThe lowest course handicap plays to 0; everyone else gets the difference. SO Low in the shipped build.
Net %100%

The lowest-handicap player plays to 0. Every other player gets one stroke on each hole whose stroke index is ≤ their (own HCP − low HCP), scaled by Net %.

No strokes given. Every segment is decided on gross scores, so there is no percentage to set and no combined allowance for the alt-shot segment.
Foursomes (alt-shot) handicap

Combined team handicap for the alt-shot segment, holes 7–12. USGA default is 50% low + 50% high.

Standard

What that means for a golfer
