Built & merged — Aug 2026

Cup leaderboard — mixed cup

A two-game mixed cup (Four Ball Nassau + Singles Nassau) gets a tab per game. Drawn to match what shipped in the Aug live-testing pass, so the pack stays true to the build.

One Stroke Play tab

  • The blank championship Stroke Play tab is gone on cup rounds — the cup is the championship. Only the per-round scores remain, so no tab shares a name with another.

Match headers

  • Full names on both sides. “Al Bronson & Chris Kang vs Alex Brown & Rob Sanders” — initials alone produced “AB vs AB”.
  • The Singles card’s separate roster row was dropped: the per-match rows already name everyone in the group.

Round Progress grid

  • Each match card now carries the per-hole grid — Hole, Par, Index, a divider, then a row per player — the same shape score entry and sixes casual use. The card result alone left the tab reading “no additional info”.
  • Rows are labelled abbreviation + strokes issued, “AY (12)”, with full names still in the header. Mirrors the Nassau grid’s strokes-in-play convention.

Who won the hole

  • The winning side’s cells are tinted in its team colour, spanning both players of the pair so the block reads as one side rather than two scores. A halved hole is left plain — most holes halve, and tinting them would make the card a wall of colour with nothing to find.
  • It uses the net score, the same number the match is decided on, so the tint and the running result never disagree.
  • Stroke-play cards (Scramble, Rumble) carry no tint: there is no hole to win, only a total.

Stroke dots

  • The whole stroke plan shows up front, allocated by index across every hole a stroke falls — played or not. Deriving dots from gross − net meant an empty card showed almost nothing.
  • Green everywhere (colorScheme.primary). Red clashed on team-coloured rows. Team colours and won-by markers are unchanged.

Scramble reads as a card, not a match

  • Two rows, one per side — the team is the competitor, so the grid holds team scores with the team stroke dotted on the same plan as everyone else.
  • Each side carries its own thru. The two groups go out at different times and are rarely on the same hole, so a single “thru 10” on the card would be wrong for one of them.
  • The number to par is the score, in the standard convention — under par red, over par and level dark. It is what a golfer reads first, so it is the only figure set large.
  • Nothing else while it is running. A net total mid-round is a number without a reference. Gross and net are added beside the to-par figure once all 18 are in, where they are worth recording.

Open

  • Score entry’s singles progress card still shows dots on played holes only. Worth closing for consistency, not blocking.
  • See Cup leaderboard for the Triple Cup board.
