Add players to a side

Shipped step 23 is a searchable roster with checkboxes and no statement of what it is filling. Two teams drafting in turn see the same screen twice, and nothing on it says which turn this is or how many picks are left.

Decisions

  • Side size is set here, and the group count falls out of it. Nothing before this step can answer it — step 1 was asked and it was removed, step 5 shows a rate because it cannot know. The draft is the first moment a roster exists, so it is the first moment 8 a side can mean 4 groups of 4. One number, stated once, and every later step reads it.
  • It is a target, not a gate — the label says aiming for. The stepper sets what the sides are aiming at; the pips and pick 4 more measure against it, and the derived line describes the cup being built rather than the golfers currently seated. Lowering it below what is already seated unseats the extras rather than leaving a side over its cap.
  • An odd total is allowed and named. Fourteen golfers is three groups and a threesome, which is legal — so the derived line says a group will be short instead of blocking a real roster.
  • The side is set by the entry point, not chosen here. The draft board already has an Add per team, so arriving on this screen has already answered which side is being filled. A switcher inside the picker would be a second control for a question that was just asked — and it would need a rule for picks held against the side you are leaving. The header names the side instead, so a captain who tapped the wrong Add sees it before he picks.
  • It is a two-column grid, not a row, so three and four sides wrap to a second line instead of clipping their names. A single row cannot carry four team names at this width. Switch the count above to see it — the roster cap follows, 8 a side at two teams and 4 at four.
  • Badge colour comes from the team, not a stylesheet. Step 3 stores one of six named colours, so any fixed set of colour classes would leave a side with an invisible badge. The name resolves through the palette into the chip, the border and the pips.
  • Picks are held, then committed. Tapping a golfer stages him — ghosted pip, a +n holding line on the count, and nothing written. Add is what puts them on the roster. A draft is a sequence of deliberate moves and a captain reads the group he is about to hand over before handing it over, so per-tap commit would be the wrong model even though it is fewer taps.
  • Held picks do not survive leaving. Backing out with picks in hand discards them, because a selection means “these go to the Bandits” and that sentence is only true on this screen.
  • The step number is on the screen. This is 6 of 7, and the draft is the step most likely to be entered from a link rather than walked to — so it has to say where it sits.
  • Entitlement is the only thing this screen sets. It answers who is in the tournament and on which side. Not which group, not which round — those belong to the builder, and keeping them out is what stops the third duplicate list.
  • Taken golfers stay visible. Hiding them makes a captain search for a name that is not there and doubt the roster. Locked with On Blue attached answers the question in place.
  • The pip row is the roster, filled to the current count and ghosted for this selection — so the effect of the picks in hand is visible before committing.
  • Selection caps at the remaining slots, then explains itself rather than silently ignoring taps. A captain who wanted a different fourth deselects one.
  • Unassigned is the default filter because it is the working set; All golfers exists for checking where somebody went.

Still open

  • Whether the draft is snake or free assignment. This screen works for both, but only snake needs a your pick turn indicator.
  • A golfer not in My Golfers yet needs an inline create. The row is easy; the handicap lookup behind it is not.
