Points by round

The shipped step asks you to type in how many groups a round has, three screens before the app knows. Groups are built at round setup, from the players who actually show up — so the number entered here is a guess, and every total computed from it is a guess too.

Groups are derived, never entered

  • The stepper is gone. Group count is read from round setup and shown with its source, so it cannot disagree with reality.
  • Two honest states. Before a round’s groups exist, the card commits to what it can — 4 pts per group — and says plainly where the total comes from. Once groups are set, the same card resolves to 12 points.
  • Because the total is derived, adding a fifth group at the course on the morning of the round updates the cup instead of contradicting a number typed a week earlier.

The arithmetic is shown as arithmetic

“Pts/win: 1” hides the thing a captain is trying to decide. Triple Cup plays four segments, so 1 pt per segment is 4 points a group — and across three groups it is 12. The card now spells that out and updates as you change the points value.

  • Segments are named, not just counted. Seeing Fourball · Foursomes · Singles 1 · Singles 2 is what makes “× 4” obvious.
  • One phrase for the unit. The build currently says Pts/win here, 1 pt per win on the group card and 1 pt/seg on the leaderboard. It is per segment; that is what it should say everywhere.
  • A cup total closes the screen, and carries the clinch line the leaderboard uses — the number the whole format hangs on, computed once rather than in the captain’s head.
Wizard state

Points per group is knowable. Totals are not.

This step runs at 5 of 7, and the draft is 6 — so when a captain first sees it there is no roster, no side sizes and no groups. Anything multiplied by a group count is invented, which is the same error as the shipped screen asking for that count outright, just quieter.

  • What the step can answer: the format, the segment order, the points a segment is worth, and therefore points per group. Four points a group is a real fact about a Triple Cup round.
  • What it cannot: the round total, the cup total, and what it takes to win. Those wait for the group count, which the draft derives from side size one step later — and say so.
  • Revisiting fills it in. The wizard is not the only way in — a captain who has built round 1’s foursomes comes back here to adjust points and sees real totals, with the group count as a link to its source. Switch the state above to see it.
  • So the honest reading of this screen is a rate, not a total — and the arithmetic strip shows the missing factor as ? rather than hiding that a multiplication is pending.

Carried over from casual Triple Cup

Casual Triple Cup already collects two things this step was missing, so the tournament reuses them rather than inventing a second model:

  • Segment order, with the hole ranges spelled out and a Play Foursomes first toggle. The ranges stay fixed at 1–6, 7–12 and 13–18 — the toggle swaps which format owns the first two, not where the boundaries fall. That is why it sits inside the round card: it is a property of a Triple Cup round, and a Singles round has nothing to order.
  • Stake, one payout for the whole cup. It does not show a cup total here: the draft is step 6, so no sides exist yet and the multiplier would be invented. It says so in the same shape the groups-pending card uses one card above — the point of this step is to stop asserting numbers it cannot know.

The handicap step takes the other three casual controls — mode, Net %, and the combined foursomes allowance.

Still open

  • Whether segment order should be settable per round or once for the cup. Drawn per round, because a two-day cup that opens with alt-shot on Saturday and fourball on Sunday is a real thing — but it doubles a control most captains touch once.
  • Whether stake belongs on this step at all. It is money rather than format, and a cup that plays for nothing should not have to see the field. Left here because the casual setup keeps them together and splitting them would add a step.
  • Whether an unresolved round should block Next. Shown here as informational, on the assumption that a captain planning two rounds should not be forced to build foursomes for round two.
