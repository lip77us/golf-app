Attach a group game

The missing prerequisite for group bets. That tab settles skins, Nassau and rabbit from gross already entered — but nothing in the cup flow lets a foursome have such a game in the first place, so it renders over an empty source.

Nothing new is needed underneath. The per-group game machinery already exists, and so does the control that decides a game’s reach: the Attach to option that multi-group skins uses. This screen extends that one option to reach a tournament, and puts an entry point on the round hub.

EntryPlayer-initiated, from the group’s own card on the round hub — not a TD control and not a wizard step.
WhoAny member of that group. The same auth that lets someone in the group write scores.
ReachOne Attach to option: this group, several groups, or the tournament. Never two.
SettlesFrom gross already entered. No second scorecard.

Decisions

  • Reach is one option, not a set of switches. Multi-group skins already asks a game how far it reaches; a tournament is one more answer to that same question rather than a second question beside it. Two independent controls would let a game be attached to four groups and the tournament, which has no meaning — the pot would be counted twice and the two settlements would disagree. A single-select makes that state unreachable instead of validating against it after the fact.
  • The tournament option is not a TD power. Attaching to the tournament widens who a foursome is playing against; it does not change anything the TD set. The cup, its points and its rounds are untouched. So it stays with the group, gated to a member of the group, and the TD is not asked to approve a wager he is not in.
  • The entry point is the group’s own card. A game belongs to the foursome, so it starts where the foursome is — the card on the round hub that already says your group. Putting it in the wizard would make the TD answer for every foursome; putting it on the leaderboard would ask people to set a bet from the screen that reports it.
  • The scope list names groups, not a count. Several groups means which ones, and money makes that precise: a golfer needs to read the names before he is in for the pot. The list is every group in the round with its tee time, and the group you are in is checked and locked.
  • Tournament reach states its own size. Choosing it says how many groups and how many golfers it will take in, because the tournament is an abstraction and the pot is not.
  • The field-wide side game stays separate. Irish Rumble or Pink Ball is the TD’s, set in the wizard, played by every foursome by instruction. This is the opposite: opted into by the people paying for it. They can coexist, and neither one names the other.
  • Settlement reads gross that is already there. Nothing on this screen asks for a second card — that is what makes attaching cheap enough to do on the first tee.

Still open

  • Whether a golfer can decline after the game is attached. Somebody in the group sets it for the group, which is how it works on the tee — but the app makes it durable in a way a handshake is not.
  • Net skins on a tournament-wide attach needs the stroke dots repeated per group; the bets tab carries the same open question.
  • Whether attaching after the round is under way should be allowed, and from which hole it counts.
