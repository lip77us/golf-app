Team score entry

Scramble, Foursomes and Chapman all put one ball in play per side. There is no per-golfer score to collect, so the row stops being a golfer and becomes a side.

Everything else stays where a player already expects it: the hole header, the score box, the inline picker under the row being edited, the match state at the bottom. Only the row label changes.

How many rows

Foursomes
Chapman		2 rows. Both pairs are in the group — four golfers, two balls, and the match is decided on this phone.
Scramble
Irish Rumble		1 row. The group is one side. The four it plays against is a separate group on a separate phone, so there is no second score to enter.

Scramble and Rumble are stroke play

  • There is no hole-by-hole match to run when the two sides are in different groups. The card keeps a running net total and the opposing group sits on the line below it, named and in its own colour, so a side playing an hour ahead can see where it stands. Net only — gross is not the competition and both numbers on one line is one too many.
  • Points are for the win, not per stroke. Stroke play here decides a winner; it does not pay by margin. A per-stroke price would make the cup total unknowable until the last card is in, and the number a captain is playing toward — 16½ to win — is the thing the whole format exists to produce. Low net total takes the match’s points, and a tie halves them.

What the row carries

  • The side’s name and its members, team colour first. A four is listed in initials, a pair in full — the row has to be readable by whoever is holding the phone, who may not be in it.
  • The stroke, as a chip and a dot — nothing else. The row is the casual singles row with a pair’s name in place of a golfer’s: name, gets 1, score box. The two combined handicaps decide who receives; how that was arrived at belongs on the group’s setup screen, not on every hole.
  • A dot above the score, the same mark score entry uses everywhere else, rather than a written-out gross 5 · net 4. The dot already says a stroke fell here; spelling out the subtraction is a second explanation of one fact.
  • One box, tapped to open the picker below the row. Identical to individual entry, so the gesture carries over untouched.

Deliberately not tracked

  • Drives used in a scramble, whose turn it is in Foursomes, which ball was picked in Chapman. These are rules the group polices at the tee; recording them adds a tap on every hole and changes no score.
  • The format line under the title states the rule as a reminder — not an enforcement, and never a blocking prompt.

Open

  • Score entry’s singles progress card still shows stroke dots on played holes only — the leaderboard shows the full plan. Worth aligning.
