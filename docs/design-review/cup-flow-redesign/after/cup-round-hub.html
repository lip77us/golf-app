Cup round hub — team identity

The shipped screen carries team membership in the colour of the golfer’s name. That fails twice: red-on-white and blue-on-white are the hardest pair for a colour-blind reader to separate, and it forces every name into a non-standard ink so nothing else on the row can use colour.

Viewing as

What changed

  • Teams get names. The wizard lets you pick colours but never a name, so every screen says “Team 1 / Team 2”. Captain-derived names are what people actually say out loud.
  • A chip carries the team — colour plus the team initial, so the signal survives greyscale and print. Names return to normal ink.
  • Aligned columns for tee and course handicap instead of a ragged right edge. Tee names normalise to one casing (the shipped screen mixes White and RED), and playing handicap only appears when it differs from CH.
  • The row acts according to who is looking. A player gets Enter Scores on his own foursome and a quiet View card on the rest, so the absence of a button no longer reads as a bug. A tournament director gets Enter Scores on every group — he is the one who fixes a card nobody in the group filled in, and a round where one foursome never entered anything is his problem to solve. Switch the role at left.
  • Your group stays marked either way. The mint border and YOUR GROUP badge are about where you are playing, not about what you may touch — a TD who is also in the field needs both facts at once.
  • Complete Round is the TD’s alone. Entering a card and closing a round are different powers: the first is data a player owns for his own group, the second ends the round for everybody.
  • The round header collapses from three stacked rows of chrome to a title, one line of detail, and the live cup tally — so the score is on the screen you are already on.

Still open

  • Team names need a length cap; two long captain names won’t fit the tally row side by side.
  • Chip initial collides when two teams start with the same letter — fall back to 1/2 or a pattern.
  • Whether a TD editing another group’s card should be attributed. Silent edits are simpler; a card that changed after the group signed it is the kind of thing people argue about.
