Cup review — final step

Review is the moment a captain decides whether the last four screens did what they meant. The shipped one spends most of its height on nothing: a Foursomes heading with no body, a Tournament Games section holding a single chip, and the line 0 players → 0 group(s) — which is a true statement about an unfinished setup, delivered as if it were a summary.

What changed

  • Nothing empty renders. A heading with no content reads as a loading failure. If there is nothing to show, the section is not drawn.
  • Unfinished setup becomes a task, not a statistic. “0 players” is replaced by a tappable row that names what is missing and where to fix it. Players can also legitimately arrive later, so it warns rather than blocks.
  • The subtitle names the button it refers to. The shipped copy says ‘Tap “Create Round”’ above a button labelled Save Tournament.
  • The button says where it goes. Save Tournament reads like the end of setup; eight required steps follow it. Save & draft teams plus a three-line list of what remains means nobody arrives at the draft screen thinking they were finished.
  • Teams appear with their names, badges and colours — the one place to catch a wrong colour before it propagates through every scorecard.
  • Rounds are listed as rounds, each with course, date and the game it plays, because that is the thing most likely to be wrong and least visible in the wizard.
  • A short ready line at the end states what will exist after saving. Review should end in a prediction, not a shrug.

Still open

  • Whether an odd player count should warn here as well — cup formats need even teams, and the draft is the place that discovers it.
