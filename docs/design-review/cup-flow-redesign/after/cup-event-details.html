Event details

The shipped wizard asks for basics at 13 and round details at 15, and the two overlap: course and date appear twice, and the second copy adds a players per round field nothing can answer yet. Merged, they become one screen with a clearer job — naming the event and establishing how many rounds there are, which is the fact the rest of the flow keeps needing.

Was13 Basics (name, course, date) then 15 Round details (course, date, players per round).
NowOne screen. Name, a course chosen from the picker — home course, a recent, or search — and a round per date.
DroppedPlayers per round. Unknowable here — the draft sets entitlement and the group builder sets who plays which round.

Decisions

  • Rounds are created here, by date. Every later step is “per round” — format, points, groups, tee times — so the round count has to exist before any of them. Making it a list of dates rather than a number means the round always has a day attached.
  • The course is inherited, not repeated. One course covers most cups; a round that moves gets its own on the round row. Shipped 15 asks again every time, which reads as though the answer might differ and usually does not.
  • The picker is the casual-round one, unchanged — search, home course, recents. A course is a course whether it is a Tuesday nine or a two-day cup, and a captain who has used the app once already knows this list.
  • Nothing is prefilled. A course sitting in the field is read as decided, so a wrong one survives to the first tee. The same reason casual rounds ask: the home course is one tap away, which is cheaper than an error nobody looked at. Next stays disabled until it is answered.
  • The round row shows the resolved name rather than “same as home”, so nobody has to hold the inheritance in their head to read what they are playing — and it reads Not set until the event course is chosen.
  • Two rounds on one day is allowed — morning and afternoon is normal for a cup — so dates are not unique keys. The row numbers the round; the date describes it.
  • The derived panel is the contract. It states what the rest of the wizard will now ask for: this many format setups, this many group builds, this many steps left. The shipped flow never tells you that multiplication is coming.
  • The step count is corrected here, not broken here. Step 1 counts for one round and says so, marking the two steps that repeat. Adding a second round moves the header from 7 to 9 in front of the captain, which is a different thing from the shipped wizard saying 4 and meaning 12.
  • No player count anywhere on this screen. That is the single field whose removal fixes the duplication we traced through three screens.

Still open

  • The picker’s action verb. In casual rounds it reads Play here, which starts a round. Here it only sets a field, so it reads Use — worth confirming that is the right divergence rather than making both say the neutral thing.
  • Whether the tee interval belongs here or on the course. The group builder treats it as a course property remembered between events, which is right, but a captain playing an unfamiliar course has to find it somewhere.
  • Round names. Round 1 is fine for a two-day cup; a Ryder-style event wants Friday Fourballs. Cheap to add, easy to leave empty.
  • Whether deleting a round after groups are built should be possible at all, or only before the first tee time is set.
