Survivor against the 160pt ceiling

The card as drawn measures ~227pt, and ~247pt on a ribbon state. The ceiling is ~160. This was never a shave — it is a block over budget, which is why layout priority could not rescue the footer.

The device, this morning

Both edges cut at once. The gold ribbon survives as a sliver above the header; Paul’s lane is sliced horizontally; the footer — $5 a Survivor and the locked THRU — is not there at all. Every field was in the payload.

The locked corners take 80pt before any Survivor content exists, leaving ~80. The headline block spends 82 on its own. The track is 65. On a ribbon state there are 60.

So the two do not compete for a margin — either one alone fills the card. The track was never marginally over; it never fit.

The recovery nobody has spent yet

The who line — PAUL LIPKIN — is 19pt telling the reader his own name on his own lock screen. The sides line already names people. Dropping it is the one cut with no cost, and it is what lets the ribbon state fit without touching the word.

Shrinking the word recovers nothing

Measured on the built cards: the headline row is 34pt whether the word is 36 or 26. The state slot beside it — 2 IN over SAM IS OUT — is 32pt on its own, and it sets the floor of that row.

So the 36→26 cut made at midnight bought no height at all. It made the word smaller for free. The height was always in the cells and the spacing; the word was never paying for anything.

Which leaves one option, not two

Protect the word lands at 135pt, or 153 with the ribbon, and keeps every slot the packet specifies except the track — which moves to the expanded island, where there is room for the whole thing at full size.

Protect the track does not fit. Not at 9pt cells, not with the sides line gone, not with the ribbon suppressed. Its smallest honest form measures 167 — still 7 over, and the column at right draws it crossing the line.

The track can only be bought by cutting the state count or a locked corner. Both are worse than moving it.

Before the sweep

Sixes, Nassau, Skins and Rabbit each gain the same 80pt of locked chrome. Worth running this arithmetic on all four on paper first — the ceiling is a number, and every one of those cards can be checked against it without a phone.

Why the alternative cannot be paid for

The word dropping to 26 is not a cost, because it is not a payment — the row stays 34pt either way. Nothing was bought, and the sides line was spent for nothing.

A three-lane track is 59pt at 9pt cells and 65 at 11 — four rows of type, so cell size barely moves it. There is no 59 in a card with 80pt of locked chrome and a 34pt headline row that will not give any back.
