Sequoya 3s, settling up

This is a foursome game with no pot and no tournament director, so settlement is not an accounting exercise — it is four men in a car park working out who hands cash to whom. The screen is built to end that conversation in one look.

Nets are real; pairwise debts are not

The stake is a man a match: lose a match carrying $10 of bets and you are down $10, and your partner is down $10. Nothing in the format says which winner your ten dollars belongs to — two losers owe twenty and two winners are owed ten each, and any pairing of those is arbitrary.

So the app never invents one. Only the four nets are real, and the payment plan below them is presented as what it is: the shortest way to make those nets true, not a record of who beat whom.

NetsFour numbers, summing to zero. This is the settled truth and the only thing the receipt asserts.
PaymentsDerived. Fewest transfers that clear the nets — for four golfers that is almost always two.
Not shownAny claim that Dave’s money is Paul’s because Paul beat him. It happens to be true here and the app must not rely on it.

Two payments, not four

A naive settlement would list every match as a transfer — six matches times four golfers is a page of five and ten dollar movements that nobody is going to execute. The screen collapses it to the fewest handovers that balance, and each one gets a tick so the group can work down the list.

The ticks are local and cosmetic. The app does not move money and does not pretend to; marking a payment settled records that the golfers said so.

The receipt is the argument-ender

One net at the top, then every match that produced it with its pairing, its margin and the bets it carried. In this format the dispute is nearly always about a press — I never agreed to that — so the bet count is on the line and a press covering different holes than the match gets its own tinted row rather than being folded into it.

The halved match stays on the receipt at zero. Six matches were played; a receipt showing five lines invites the question the receipt exists to prevent.

Presses carry the time they were taken

An auto press has no author, so it reads auto, first hole won. A hand-called press reads called by Paul, 6th tee. That is the difference between a rule and a decision, and only one of them is arguable — naming who called it is what makes the argument short.

It leaves the app as plain text

Same rule as the tournament receipt: label, en dash, amount, no columns, no links. The group thread gets the nets and the two payments; nobody needs six match lines in a text message.

Still open

  • Whether to offer a running tab across a regular game — the same four golfers every Saturday, settling monthly. It changes this screen from a receipt into a statement.
  • Whether a golfer can dispute a press from here, or only in the moment. Drawn as only in the moment: an offer that expires on the tee cannot be reopened at the bar.
