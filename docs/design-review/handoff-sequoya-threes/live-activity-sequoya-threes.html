Sequoya 3s on the lock screen

Structurally this is the Sixes card and deliberately so — same three rows, same locked corners, same neutral scoreboard. Two things are different, and both come from the three-hole match: the wager moves, and there is far more news.

The press rides with the money, not the headline

A match that quietly picks up a second bet is the one number on this card the reader cannot derive from anything else. It could not go in the top row — the right half of that row is locked to hole, par, yardage across the whole set — and it must not go in the headline, which belongs to the match state.

So it sits in the footer as a chip beside the total at risk: $10 a man, + auto press. The cause is printed with the effect, which is the whole reason the chip exists. A press is never a doubling of the match bet — it is a second bet at the same amount over the holes that are left — and money that has silently appeared is worse than no money on the card at all.

Six pushes minimum, and they are not all the same

Sixes gets a push when the pairing changes. This format changes pairing five times on a schedule and opens a fresh bet up to six more times, so the discipline has to be tighter or the round becomes a stream.

New pairingFive a round, on the 4th, 7th, 10th, 13th and 16th tees. Mint. Names both new sides.
Press offeredAmber, and the only push that expires. Sent to all four; says which two can act.
Auto pressNo push. It carries no decision, so it rides the activity update and the footer chip.

The auto press is the interesting exclusion. It opens a bet without asking, which sounds like exactly what deserves a push — but it fires on every won opening hole, which is most matches, and a notification that arrives six times a round to report a rule the group already agreed to is noise. The chip in the footer is the receipt; the push is for decisions.

The state slot is the match, same as Sixes

The slot takes a word only when there is one. DORMIE means up by exactly the holes remaining — one up with one to play, two up with two — and over three holes that is the common case rather than the dramatic one, so it reads as ordinary here. When the match closes early the slot says CLOSED and the headline holds the margin: 2 & 1. Anything else takes an em dash over the holes left, because a state word that is not true of the position is worse than no word: 1 UP with 2 to play is not dormie and must never be labelled it.

The press offer does not take this slot. A Live Activity that shows a live offer with no way to accept it is worse than one that stays quiet; the push does that job and the card stays a glance.

Six pips are honest here, and still do not ship on the card

Sixes cut segment pips because a round has no fixed number of matches. This round has exactly six, always, so six pips would be true — a real difference between the formats.

They stay off the lock card anyway, for a different reason: the card has no room for a fourth row. The panel is built to a hard ceiling and the pips would cost more than they return, since the top row already names the match and the footer already carries the money. They ship in the expanded island, where there is no footer — and there they earn it, because the second half repeats the first and a six-pip strip is where a golfer sees he is about to replay the pairing that beat him.

The final state names the format’s question

Not just the money. Won 3, lost 2, halved 1 and the partner who carried you — the one thing this format produces that no other in the app does, because you played every partner twice and the round has an answer to who.

Observers get the same card, minus two slots

A golfer who was invited to follow the round but could not play gets the leaderboard and, if they want it, this activity. No new card is needed — the neutral-scoreboard rule already did the work: the three rows name both pairs and never say you, so an observer reads the identical composition.

Two slots are personal and come out:

MoneyOmitted. The footer keeps the total at risk and the press chip — what the hole is worth is part of the match — but the running +$10 is the reader’s own position and an observer has none.
Score to parOmitted. THRU X · ±Y is the reader’s round. An observer keeps THRU X alone, which the locked corner already supports — it is the round’s position, not a personal one.
Final stateReads the match record and who won the round rather than collect from Dave. Same layout, different sentence.

Everything else is identical, including the pushes: an observer wants the pairing changes most of all, since they are the one thing that cannot be inferred. The press offer should not push to observers — it is a decision addressed to two golfers, and buzzing a spectator about a bet they cannot take is noise.

If this generalises across the set it belongs in the pattern as a rule on the money and par slots, not as a card per game.

Still open

  • Whether the press-offered push should be silent for the pair who cannot act. Drawn as the same push to all four — being pressed without being told is worse than one extra buzz.
  • Whether a press taken should itself push a confirmation. Drawn as no: the footer chip and the total at risk both move, and that is the receipt.
