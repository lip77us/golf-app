Sequoya 3s, leaderboard

Four golfers, six matches, every pairing twice. There is no team standing to show because the teams do not persist, and no useful individual ranking either — so this board is the round as it happened, in order, then one table of money.

A ledger, not a standing

The earlier draft opened as four golfer rows you tapped to expand. It answered how did I do six times over and never once answered what happened in this round, which is the question a foursome actually asks at the bar. Every golfer’s card repeated the same six matches from a different side.

So the board reads chronologically. Match 1 through match 6, one line per bet, then the table. A match without a press is a single line; a match with one is two. Nothing is per-viewer; four phones show the same screen.

Match blockOne line per bet, nothing else. No match header, no pairing row — the bet line already names the match, its holes, its margin and the pair that won it.
Press linesTinted, and labelled with cause on the same line: auto has no author, called by Paul does.
TableSix match rows × four golfers, signed, then the total. Every column sums; the rows sum to zero across.

The press is the dispute surface, so it gets its own line

A press is a new bet at the same amount, never a doubling, so it settles on its own holes and can go the other way. All three cases are in this round: match 2 is lost 2 & 1 by Paul and Sam, who then take the hole-6 press off Dave and Lee; match 6 is won with its press halved; match 5 is won and Dave’s closing press is halved too. A board that folded presses into a match total would report each of those as one number and make the difference impossible to see.

The card underneath is where that comes from. Gross scores are the source of truth — strokes come off by index, and every bet result on this screen is computed from them. Nothing about a bet is entered by hand.

The boxed cell is the score that won the hole for its side. A halved hole boxes nothing and the losing side’s counting net is never boxed — one box a hole, or none. That is what makes the bet lines above countable rather than merely asserted: a golfer disputing a press can count boxes across its two holes.

It scrolls sideways rather than shrinking to fit. Eighteen columns at a legible size do not fit a phone, and a card you cannot read is worse than one you have to push.

Every bet result here is derived from the hole scores, not entered. A press over holes 5–6 and a match over 4–6 are decided by the same three holes, so the app must not let a press result contradict the match it sits inside — that is a validation rule, not a display rule.

One table, four columns, sums to zero

The money table is the settlement in miniature. Each row is a match net per golfer; the bottom row is what each man collects or pays. The row totals sum to zero and the app says so — a foursome will check, and an assertion that fails that check is worse than no table.

Partners are visible without being labelled: in any row two columns carry the same number. That is the format, and printing with Dave on every cell would triple the ink to say it.

Mid-round it shows the same thing, shorter

Closed matches only. A match in progress appears as its block with live bet states and no money, and its table row is dashes. The match number is the position in the round — match 5 of 6 says more than thru 14.

Still open

  • Whether the partner record (every golfer partners every other exactly twice) belongs here at all now that the board is chronological. It is the format’s own interesting question but it is not money. Drawn as not here — it lives on the settlement receipt.
  • Whether the table should be sortable by column. Drawn as no: four columns, and sorting a ledger destroys the only order it has.
