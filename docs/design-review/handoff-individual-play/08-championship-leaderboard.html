Championship leaderboard

The tab that pays the 36-hole money. Live shows a headline and, when a row is opened, a two-column fragment — hole 1 and an empty Out. Opened here it is all eighteen: par, stroke index, gross with a dot per stroke received, and the net the board is actually ranking.

Every round gets a column. The counting rule sits in the chip strip — here best 4 of 6. The dropped rounds are struck through on the row rather than hidden, so a golfer can see what he is throwing away, and they move as scores land.

Four columns, then the strip scrolls. Six rounds will not fit beside a name and a total at 390px, so the round columns are their own horizontal strip: every row scrolls together with the header, and it opens scrolled to the most recent round — which is the one anybody opening the board mid-tournament came to see. The earlier rounds are a swipe to the right.

Why it matters: on a multi-day 36-hole outing a golfer may sit a round out, and best 3 of 4 or best 4 of 6 keeps him in the championship.

A round in progress does not compete for a place in the count. A partial round nearly always looks like a good one — four holes at level par would knock out a finished 73 — so the live round joins the selection only when it is complete. It is still shown, in amber, and its holes are still on the card.

Both scoring methods use the same card. Stableford shows gross and points, and no per-hole net — a golfer who wants his 18-hole net has the gross and the stroke allocation right there and can take it off himself. One method, one board: a Stableford tournament does not also draw a net stroke-play board.

What the dots mean

One dotOne stroke on that hole, by stroke index against course handicap.
Two dotsA second stroke — a 38 index gets one on all 18 and a second on SI 1–20.
Tinted cellThe cap bit. Gross was higher; the hole counts at net double bogey.

The cap is visible, not silent

An 10 on the par 5 counts as an 8 — par + 2 plus the stroke. Live applies this and shows nothing, so a golfer adding his own card gets a different number than the board. The tinted cell and the footnote are the whole fix.

Money is a projection

Prize sits in the muted italic weight all round, with one line under the table. It goes to full weight when the round closes. A golfer thru 1 showing $48 in green is the single most misleading thing on the live board.

Ranking

Ties share a position and carry T, and split the money for the places they occupy. Thru sits under the name, because a net score without its holes is not comparable — see finding 5 on the play diagnosis.

The tabs read the tournament

Every tab is the name the TD set, not a hardcoded one: Mini Singles Bracket, and the ball game as typed — here Beer Ball, because that is what this group called it. The last tab is the day bet for the round being played.
