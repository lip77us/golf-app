Settlement

Every money decision in this spec lands here. Seven pots over two days, entries taken at signup and prizes that only resolved when the last card came in — and what a golfer wants is one number: does he pay or does he collect.

One net number, itemised underneath

The row is the answer; the card is the proof. Open it and every line that produced the number is there — each entry as a debit, each prize as a credit, each with the game that caused it.

CollectsGreen, listed first. Sorted by amount, because the man owed the most wants to see it first.
PaysMuted. Everybody who entered everything and won none is here, and the total is always exactly what he staked.
Two placesThe championship pays 1st and 2nd here, which is the TD’s call rather than a limit. The only rule is that the last paying place clears the day bet’s 1st — $100 in this tournament — so the 36-hole DQ never costs a man money. A third place at $100 would also pass.
Group prizesShown as the golfer’s share, with the group named — Irish Rumble, Group 2 (3 ways) — because a share is the only part he can check.

Two games run twice

Irish Rumble and the ball game are re-run every round, so each appears here as two pots — R1 and R2, entered separately, won separately. A group that wins the ball on both days collects twice, and Group 1 does exactly that.

Not everybody staked the same

Six of the sixteen are out of the day bet, for two different reasons, and neither pays into it. The four Mini Singles finalists are playing a match rather than posting a stroke card; the two championship money winners are disqualified from it by winning. All six stake $100 and list six entries; the ten who can win it stake $120.

Foursome side bets are not here, and that is deliberate

What settles on this screen is the tournament-scope games only — the five the TD set. Foursome side bets — skins, Nassau, spots, rabbit — are settled inside the foursome and never appear in these totals.

The reason is ownership: the TD did not set them, does not know the stakes, and is not collecting for them. Folding them in would make him responsible for money he never handled, and would make a golfer’s net number depend on a bet three of his playing partners agreed to on the first tee.

Nothing is paid until the pools balance

The By game view is the TD’s check, not the golfer’s: entries in, prizes out, and the difference. A game that does not balance blocks the whole settlement and says which one — the mistake is always in a payout table, and it is always found faster by naming the game than by naming a dollar figure.

The two carve-outs make this less obvious than it sounds: Mini Singles day 2 takes $160 out of the championship pool rather than charging an entry, so the championship shows $640 in and $640 out with $160 of that leaving for another game’s table.

The day bet resolves last

It cannot settle until the championship does — the golfers in the 36-hole money are out of it, and that is only known at the end. So the day bet line stays provisional and the Settle button stays off until every round is closed.

Sum zero, or it is wrong

The footer is the one number that proves the whole screen: collected minus paid must be zero. It is a closed system — the golfers fund it entirely — so any other answer is an arithmetic bug, not a rounding preference.
