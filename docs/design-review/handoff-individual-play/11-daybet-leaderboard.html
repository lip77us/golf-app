Day bet — the final round’s 18-hole stroke play side bet

The last day’s 18-hole stroke play side bet: a separate pot on that round alone, and the only board in the set whose result is not knowable while it is being played. Anyone who wins 36-hole money is out of it — and nobody knows who that is until the championship closes.

Two ways out, and only one of them is italic

The last round strips this bet down twice over, which is what sizes the pool and the places.

Not here at allThe four Mini Singles finalists. They are playing a match, not posting a stroke card, so they are neither charged nor ranked. An absence, not an exclusion — no italic row.
Here but italicThe championship money winners. They play the round and appear on the board, but cannot collect — and do not contribute: their entry is returned at settlement. Only known when the championship closes, which is why they stay visible until then.
What is leftSixteen − four − two = ten, who both fund the pot and can win it. Ten pays three places; a smaller field drops to two, then one. The places scale with the round, not the entry list.

Provisional, in italic

The temptation is to hide the ineligible. That makes the board jump at the end with no explanation, so instead they stay in place, in italic, with one line at the top saying what the italic means.

The pool is provisional for the same reason. It reads $200 from ten on the assumption the current 36-hole leaders hold; if somebody else wins that money instead, one entry comes back in and another goes out, and the two places resize. Nothing is collected until the championship closes, so no refund is ever actually handed back.

Italic rowCurrently in the 36-hole money, and the subtitle says where — 1st, 2nd, 3rd. Drops out if he finishes there, so his day-bet position is provisional.
Upright rowOut of contention for the 36-hole win, which is precisely who this bet is for.
PrizeShown against the first three eligible golfers, not the first three rows. An italic above them is the normal case.
TiesShare a position, carry T, and split the money for the places they occupy — the same rule as every other board. Ineligible rows hold a position but do not consume a paid place.

Which is the whole point of the game: it pays a great single round from somebody with nothing left to play for. Brown shooting 68 on Sunday from twelfth place is the man it exists for.

Gross or net follows the tournament

The DQ reads the tournament’s own handicap setting rather than naming a scoring type. This is a net event, so the line reads “Winners of 36-hole net money are not eligible” and the italics mark the current net leaders.

The DQ must not cost a man money

The two pots have to be sized against each other. If the last paying place in the 36-hole championship is worth less than 1st in the day bet, then finishing in the championship money actively costs a golfer money — the placing disqualifies him from a bigger cheque. Nobody should be worse off for playing better.

So the payout step checks it: last paying championship place ≥ day bet 1st. Here the championship pays two places, so that is 2nd at $200 against $140 here — comfortably clear. A setup-time check, not a play-time one, but worth stating on the board that depends on it.

Thru beside the name, projected money in muted italic, all 18 with stroke dots and the cap shown when the row is opened — the championship board’s card, unchanged.
