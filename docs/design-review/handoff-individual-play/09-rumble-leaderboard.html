Irish Rumble, leaderboard

A group ranking is only half the answer. The question a golfer actually asks is how did we get there — and in this game that means which balls counted, hole by hole. So the row opens into the full card.

Five characters, or initials

Ten columns of numbers leave about forty pixels for a name, so the card uses the short name — capped at five characters, defaulting to initials when the TD has not set one. It is the same field the group already uses elsewhere, not a new one invented for this card.

Tap the row to collapse it

The card is a disclosure, not a permanent state: tapping the open row closes it, and opening another closes the first. One card at a time, so the ranking stays on screen.

Four rows, and the net is readable

No net row. Par, four gross rows and the dots carry it: a 6 with one dot on a par 4 is a net 5, and you can see it without a fifth line of numbers. Adding net rows would double the card and halve the type.

NamesFull names on the summary row. On the card, the short name: five characters, defaulting to initials until the TD sets one. Barry Labass is Bazza because somebody typed it; Aldo Detomasi is AD because nobody did. The borrowed ball is 4th.
Header bandHole numbers on the tinted band, par under it, stroke index in the lighter row — the same three-row header the live card already uses.
DotsStrokes received on that hole, by stroke index. Same dot convention as every other card.
Group +/−The counted nets against par for that hole, so a 3 and a 4 on a par 4 reads −1 rather than 7. Totals read the same way as the ranking above.
Green cellThis ball counted. Two green cells on a hole means two nets went into the group total.
Amber outlineCapped at net double bogey — the gross shown is not the number that counted.
Blue italicThe borrowed 4th. Somebody else’s ball, playing his handicap.

No count row either. The green cells are the count — three of them on a par 3, one on a par 5 — so printing the number underneath would be saying the same thing twice on a card that has no room for it.

Where the borrowed ball shows

Group 2 plays three men and a borrowed 4th. Its row is blue and italic, and under the card a line names the donor for each hole — the rotation is fixed at setup and spread across the field, so the strip reads as eight different names rather than one shadow player.

A hole where the donor has not posted shows an empty cell, and the total is marked provisional — on the card and beside the group name, as F* in amber. Group 2 leads by one with three borrowed nets outstanding, so a golfer reading only the ranking would otherwise see a settled winner. The board does not guess, and it does not let the row imply what the card denies.

Money

Prize is italic and muted while the round is live, the same projection convention as the championship board. The place pays a group, and splits among its real golfers — three ways for a levelled group.
