Individual play — the live flow, and what changes

Read against the eight captures of the shipped build (Duddy Cup, 8 players, 2 rounds, Tilden Park). The good news first: step 1 already asks type before anything else and the derived group count, the named blocker on Select Players and the honest “x of 7” counter are all working the way the Cup redesign argued for. The problems left are about naming and ownership, not sequence — four of them are one issue wearing four hats: a game is chosen in one place, named in another, and configured in a third.

Findings

1. The header says round, the screen means tournamentNaming

Today: every step is titled New Round (x of 7), while step 2 names an event with two rounds and step 7 ends in Create Round — which then creates both.

Call it New Tournament. A round is a thing inside the tournament, created for each date. This is the same fix the Cup flow needed and it costs one string per screen.

2. SO Low is offered where it has no meaningWrong option

Today: Handicap shows Net / Gross / SO Low for an Individual event.

Strokes off the low index is a Cup mechanism — it exists so a match plays off the low golfer. Against a field there is no low golfer to play off. The third option must not render for this type.

3. The cap is a rule, not a preferenceSetting

Today: a Net Double-Bogey Cap toggle, default on, buried under the allowance slider.

Always on. State it as a rule with the worked ceiling — par + 2 + strokes on the hole — so a golfer who picks up knows what lands on his card. Gross boards ignore it; net boards apply it; Stableford never needs it.

4. One game, three namesNaming

Today: the bracket is Mini Singles Bracket on the chips and at review, and Set Up Group 1 / 2 on Game Setup. In conversation it is the Match Play Foursome.

Pick one name and use it on the chip, at review, on Game Setup and on the leaderboard tab. The rules belong on the game once — a one-day bracket inside each foursome, 9 holes a match, winner takes all — with the per-group rows carrying only fee and payout.

5. “Stroke Play” means two different things on one screenAmbiguous

Today: review lists Stroke Play Championship under Tournament Games and Stroke Play under Active Games. Both are true, and nothing distinguishes them.

The second is the day bet: it inherits the main game and pays each day separately, so a two-round event has two of them. Label it by what it pays — Day bet · Round 1, Day bet · Round 2 — and state the eligibility rule where the bet is created: anyone who wins 36-hole money is disqualified from it.

Decided: gross or net follows whatever the tournament’s handicap setting is — a net event disqualifies the net money winners, a gross event the gross ones. The day bet exists to pay a great single round from someone out of contention for the 36-hole win, so the wording on the setup screen reads from the setting rather than naming a scoring type: “Winners of 36-hole net money are not eligible.”

Eligibility is not knowable until the last round ends, so the Round 2 day-bet board is provisional while it plays: show the 36-hole money leaders in place, in italics, and say once at the top that italic rows drop out if they finish in the 36-hole money. Hiding them mid-round would make the board jump at the end with no explanation.

6. Money is collected in two places, one round lateOwnership

Today: step 6 takes the championship fee and payouts. Side games are ticked there but priced afterwards, on a Game Setup checklist that appears once the round exists.

Entry is a flat fee per side game, taken at signup — so the fee belongs beside the chip that turns the game on. Then Game Setup has nothing left to collect, the field's entries are known before round one, and no golfer is asked for money after he has teed off.

7. Game Setup's radios look like choicesControl

Today: each row carries an empty radio circle and a chevron. The circles are progress, not selection — but they read as a pick list, and Done is enabled either way.

Rows show state: a tick and the fee when set, Not set when not. Done states its blocker rather than letting a round start with a picked game that has no money attached.

8. Step 6's own heading scrolls under the progress barLayout

Today: the capture shows Stroke Play Championship half-clipped behind the progress bar — the section header scrolls under it, so the fee fields lose their subject.

Splitting payouts from side games mostly fixes this. Whatever remains long enough to scroll needs its section header pinned, so a money field is never on screen without the pot it belongs to.

Side-game setup — the money model

Seven more captures, one per configure screen. The money block is already a single component — entry fee, Play for fun — no stakes, a pool line, paid places, per-place amounts, Payouts balance ✓ and Auto-suggest — and all four games balanced exactly. What it does not yet say is whose money it is: the same table pays a foursome in one game and a golfer in another, at a field pool in one and a group pool in the next.

9. Paid places never say who is paidMoney

Today: the same 1st / 2nd / 3rd table means three different things — a golfer against the field, a golfer inside one foursome, and a whole foursome whose prize is then divided.

Label the recipient on the row: 1st — foursome or 1st — golfer. The split line Pink Ball and Irish Rumble already carry (“splits to $30.00/player”) is the right instinct; it just needs to appear as the rule rather than as a footnote.

10. Pool scope is invisibleMoney

Today: Match Play’s pool is $40 (4 players) and Irish Rumble’s is $80 (8 players), in identical cards, with three different phrasings of the pool line.

One sentence, one place: “$10 × 4 in this foursome = $40” versus “$10 × 8 in the field = $80”. A captain reading two of these in a row should not have to count players to know which pot he is filling.

11. The cap is now set in three placesDuplication

Today: a Net Double-Bogey Cap toggle on wizard step 3, another on Stroke Play setup, and a note under Irish Rumble’s preview pointing at “the toggle below”.

Three switches for one rule can disagree with each other, and the leaderboard cannot show which one won. With the cap always on all three disappear and it is stated once, where scores are explained.

12. SO Low is right here and wrong on step 3Scope

Today: SO Low is offered both on the Individual handicap step and on Match Play setup, where it is selected and explained: the low golfer in the foursome plays to 0.

That is the distinction to build on. SO Low is a match mechanism — it needs an opponent group to be low within. Keep it on Match Play, delete it from the field-wide handicap step.

13. Pink Ball’s ball is red, and its rules are missingNaming

Today: Pink Ball Setup → Ball Color: Red. The only rule on the screen is that each group picks its rotation later.

Either the ball is pink or the game is not. If the colour is genuinely a choice, name the game for the mechanism and offer swatches, not free text. And state the rule where the money is: one ball, no replacements, last group to lose it wins, low net breaks a tie between groups that finish 18 with it alive.

14. The bracket preview stops half wayIncomplete

Today: Auto-seed by handicap gives 1 v 4 and 2 v 3, and both semis are labelled front 9. Nothing names the final.

Nine holes a match, winner takes all — so four golfers need semis on the front, final on the back. Show all three matches, and say what a halved match does before it happens rather than after.

Decided: a halved match plays on. The match continues past its ninth hole and the result is back-calculated against the finals pairing, reading back from hole 10. The bracket card has to carry that sentence, because it changes which holes a golfer is still playing for.

15. Save is disabled without a reasonBlocker

Today: on the first Match Play screen Save Configuration is grey and silent.

Same rule as the Cup flow: nothing is disabled without saying why. Name the missing input in place — and if the only thing missing is money, the button should say so.

16. Two games take two screens, two take onePattern

Today: Irish Rumble and Match Play split into · Game then · Money with Next / Back; Pink Ball and Stroke Play are a single screen ending in Save.

Pick one shape for all four: rules first, money last, one Save.

Decided: no game gets a second rules screen. Custom (per-hole) already reveals its 18-cell editor inline, under the mode radio on · Game — Segment Preview, Front 9 / Back 9 grid, tap to cycle 1→2→3→4. That is the pattern: the mode picker expands in place, and the only screen after it is money.

17. Keep: copy across groups, and the balance checkWorking

Copy to all match plays (1 other group) names its own count and saves the repetition a per-group game creates — the same move the Cup allowance copy needs.

Payouts balance ✓ with Auto-suggest is the strongest control in the whole flow: it turns a division problem into a confirmation. Use the identical pair on every pot, including the championship.

Proposed step list

  • 1 · Type & format — as shipped, plus the consequence strip.
  • 2 · Event details — as shipped.
  • 3 · Handicap — net or gross, allowance; cap stated as a rule; no SO Low.
  • 3a · Stableford points — the casual Stableford setup screen, reused. Only when the method is Stableford.
  • 4 · Select players — as shipped.
  • 5 · Groups & tees — as shipped, tee labels normalised.
  • 6 · Payouts — championship pot only, balance check kept.
  • 7 · Side games — Pink Ball, Irish Rumble, Match Play Foursome, and the day bet on multi-day events. Fee per game, here.
  • 8 · Review → Create tournament — one name per game, each with its fee.

Eight steps counted, seven shown for a one-round stroke-play event — the same arithmetic the Cup step does. Nothing above changes the order a captain already walks; it renames three things, moves the side-game fees one step earlier, and deletes one option that should never have been on this type.

Still open: whether tee times belong on Groups & Tees or stay on the round; whether the 36-hole DQ counts gross winners, net winners, or both; whether a halved Match Play match splits or plays on; and whether Pink Ball’s rotation should stay deferred to the group or be set with the fee.
