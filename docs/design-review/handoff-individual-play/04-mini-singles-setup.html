Mini Singles Bracket, setup

Optional, at the TD’s discretion. Plenty of stroke play tournaments run without it — the game is switched on at the side-game step, and this screen is only reached when it is. Nothing else assumes it exists: no carve-out is taken from the championship pool, and no foursome is reserved on day 2, unless the TD turned it on.

This is a two-stage game, and the previous draft had it wrong. It is not one bracket inside one foursome — it is a bracket in every group on day 1, and on day 2 the group winners are pulled into one foursome that plays its own bracket for the title.

Day 1Four groups, four brackets. Semis on the front, final and 3rd place on the back. Each group produces a winner.
Day 2The four winners play as one foursome — semis, then the final and a 3rd-place match. One champion.
Everyone elsePlays day 2 as a normal stroke-play round. Their match play is over.

Which is exactly why it is a side game and not the championship: run it as the main event and three quarters of the field is out of contention on Saturday afternoon.

Halved matches: the same rule both days

The distinction is not Saturday against Sunday — it is what the match still has to produce. Money can always be split; a name cannot.

SemiPlays on, back-calculated from hole 10. Both days, because a semi always has nine holes left and the golf can settle it.
FinalMoney splits between 1st and 2nd. The trophy — and the Sunday seat, on day 1 — goes to the last hole won.
3rd placeSplit. Nothing depends on the order, so nothing needs breaking.

Which makes last-hole-won a tiebreak of last resort rather than a general rule: it is used only where a trophy or a bracket seat requires exactly one name.

Money first

The order is now money, then format, then the rules to read. Two pots and a carve-out are what a TD came to this screen to set; the brackets below are mostly confirmation, and the halved-match rule is reference. Seeds sit under the money because they are pre-filled and rarely touched.

The casual payout pattern, unchanged

Casual Match Play already solved this and the tournament screen now uses it verbatim: a paid places stepper, an editable amount per place, Payouts balance ✓ on the left and Auto-suggest on the right. No preset chips — an earlier draft had three, which was a second vocabulary for a problem that already had one.

Auto-suggest proposes the same 60 / 25 / 15 casual uses — $24 / $10 / $6 on a $40 pot. Third does not get his entry back, and that is the call: the day 1 pot exists so the 3rd-place match is worth playing, not so it is free. One vocabulary, one set of percentages, across casual and tournament.

Each place shows its share of the pot beside the amount, recalculated as he types — so a TD thinking in percentages and one thinking in dollars are looking at the same row. The TD can type over any of it. Two guards, both stated in the balance line rather than blocked: the places must add to the pot, and each must pay less than the one above.

Worth checking against net play. If the existing net-play setup already ships a suggestion list, these should match it rather than introduce a fourth set of percentages — tell me what it uses and I will align them.

Four sections are closed by default

This is the longest game to set up, and most of it is right the first time. So the parts a TD rarely touches — seeds, the empty-seat rule, handicap, and the Sunday eligibility notes — sit closed, each showing its current value on the header line: By index, Promote best runner-up, Strokes off low.

Still one screen and one Save, which keeps it consistent with the other three games. Splitting it into a wizard would have made Mini Singles the only game that asks twice.

Two pots, and only one is set here

The two days are funded differently, which the previous draft had wrong — it treated the whole game as one carve-out and paid nothing on Saturday.

Day 1A side bet, entered per golfer and paid inside each group. It exists because the 3rd-place match has to be worth playing.
Day 2The carve-out, a percentage of the championship pool taken at tournament setup. Read-only here: this screen reports it, it does not set it.

So Saturday’s 3rd-place match is played for money after all, and Sunday’s three places split $160 between them.

4th takes nothing, and that is fine

He is still in the main tournament — losing a semi on Sunday costs him nothing he was otherwise going to win. The earlier draft refunded his entry; there is no entry on Sunday to refund.

The guards are stated in the balance line rather than enforced silently: the places must add to the pot, and each must pay less than the one above.

The field has to fit 16

Group count is what the game actually runs on, and golfers per group is what determines it. Four is the ceiling:

Up to 8Two groups, two winners — a final with no semis. Not a bracket; not offered.
9 to 12Three groups. Three winners, so Sunday takes one of the two shapes below.
13 to 16Four groups. The full bracket, both days identical in shape.
Over 16Five winners cannot play a knockout in one round — it needs a third day. Not an option on a two-day tournament, so the card says so rather than inventing a play-in.

Worth confirming: you said at least 13, which is the point where the field forces a fourth group and Sunday needs no special case. Drawn as 9 upward, since the three-group shapes below are yours — say the word and 13 becomes a hard floor with the three-group card removed.

A tie on points plays on

Same instinct as the halved match, and the same mechanism: nothing is decided by a card. If two or three are level after nine, points keep being played until 1st and 2nd are clear, and the finals match is then back-calculated from the 10th — the same reading-back the halved match uses, so the holes that separated them count twice: once to settle the points, once as the opening of the match.

Nobody stands around while it happens: the three are already playing the back nine, and the match they are in resolves itself behind them.

Seeding Sunday

Not index again — day 1 margin, so the man who won 5&4 gets seed 1 and meets the narrowest winner. Lowest index breaks a tie, which it will: two 2&1 winners is an ordinary Saturday.

Three groups, or a withdrawal — one rule

Two ways to end up short a man — three groups, or a winner who withdraws — and the TD answers both once, at setup, rather than being asked on Sunday morning. Drawn as a choice, defaulting to promotion because it keeps Sunday identical to every other year:

PromoteThe lowest net beaten finalist takes the empty seat, and Sunday is a normal bracket. He is not being handed anything — he still has to win two matches, same as the group winners.
Short-handedNobody is refilled. Three left play points then a match; two left play a straight nine-hole match. No byes — a seat nobody earned is not handed out as a free pass to the final.
PointsAll three play points over the front, the two leaders play a match on the back. Truer to “you get there by winning your group”, but the third man’s day ends at the turn.

Promotion needs a stated tiebreak of its own — lowest net over the day-1 round is the obvious one, and it is what the card says. The earlier worry about it weakening “you get here by winning your group” is answered by the format itself: two matches is two matches, whichever seat you came in on.

Two prizes, and they can be the same man

The match play champion takes the top of the carve-out; the stroke play winner takes what is left of the championship. Nothing disqualifies either from the other.

The finalists keep their other side games on Sunday — the ball game and Irish Rumble run as normal — but they are out of the day-2 stroke bet, since they are playing a match rather than a card against the field.

What the TD sets, and what is derived

Seeds are seeded by index and draggable per group. The day 2 foursome is not set at all — it is derived from day 1, so the card shows four TBD slots that fill themselves.

That has a consequence for the group builder: on day 2 those four golfers must be grouped together, and the TD cannot know who they are when he sets tee times. The groups step needs to reserve that foursome rather than have the TD rebuild it on the morning.

Handicap

Strokes off low is the default here, and this is the one game where that is right: a match has two players and a low man to anchor to, so the higher index plays the difference and the lower plays scratch. Full net is offered but is not what a singles match wants. Set once, and it governs both days.

It is the reverse of the field games — Irish Rumble and the ball game inherit full net from the tournament, because there is no single opponent to take strokes off against.

A halved semi — both days

Play continues into the back nine and the match is back-calculated from hole 10. Identical on Saturday and Sunday: a semi always has holes left, so the golf settles it.

Nobody waits. The other pair play hole 10 on time and score under vs. TBD — the two still level can only halve, since the first hole they do not halve names the opponent.

A halved final — both days

The money splits. 1st and 2nd are added together and shared; nobody is played off for cash.

Where a single name is needed — the trophy, and who advances to Sunday — it is the last hole won. Read the card backwards to the most recent hole either golfer took outright: Paul won 11, Jim won 13, everything else halved, Jim.

A halved 3rd-place match — both days

Split, and nothing else. 3rd and 4th share what those two places pay. No tiebreak is needed because nothing depends on the order.
