Beer Ball, leaderboard

Modelled on the Rumble board — same ranking row, same three-row card header, same disclosure. What changes is what the card has to prove: not which nets counted but who had the ball, and where it died.

The ranking is not a score

Live sorts this tab by net, which is the wrong thing entirely. The last group still holding the ball wins. A group that loses it on 3 finishes behind a group that loses it on 17 no matter how either of them scored, so the board ranks by survival first and only then by net.

Alive at 18Top of the board. If more than one survives, the ball’s own net separates them — the only time score decides anything here.
LostRanked by the hole it died on, latest first. Lost on 14 beats lost on 6.
Ball netThe carrier’s net against par — not a four-man aggregate. Shown only for groups whose ball is still alive: a ball lost on 5 covers four holes and a ball that survives covers eighteen, so printing both in one column would rank the worst group best. An eliminated group reads –, and its real total is on its card.

The card follows the ball

One ball rotating in the order the group set on the first tee, and the card makes that order legible: the carrier’s cell is highlighted on each hole, so reading down the highlights traces the ball through the round.

Under the four gross rows, a Ball row: the carrier’s net against par on that hole, and a running total for each nine. It is the only line on the card that is about the ball rather than about a golfer, and it is the number the ranking column shows — a tie at 18 is settled on the ball, not on a four-man aggregate nobody was playing for.

The hole it was lost on carries a red mark, and everything after loses its highlight — those holes were played and are still readable, they simply no longer mean anything to this game. A golfer who missed the moment can see it without asking.

Thru sits beside the group name rather than in a column of its own — amber while they are still out there — which gives the four full names the width they need. An unfinished net stays muted, because net is what separates two surviving groups and a net over sixteen holes is not comparable to one over eighteen.

Same conventions, no new ones

Full names on the summary row, short names on the card — five characters, initials by default. Stroke dots, the amber outline for a score capped at net double bogey, and the projected-money italic all carry over unchanged.

The name is the TD’s

Every string on this screen reads the name he typed. It says Beer Ball deliberately — the ball had a stein printed on it, and the name is proof the app never asks for a colour. Next month the same screen says Devil Ball or Pink Ball without a code change.
