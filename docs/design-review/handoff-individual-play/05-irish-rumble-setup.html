Irish Rumble, setup

Same shape as Match Play: rules first, money last, one Save. Live splits it into · Game then · Money; the money here is four rows, which is not a screen.

The mode picker expands in place

Your screenshot settles it: Custom (per-hole) already reveals its 18-cell editor inline under the radio, with the Segment Preview above it. So no game needs a second rules screen — the pattern is the choice opens its own detail where it was made, and the only step after rules is money. Finding 16.

What the cap note becomes

Live carries “each score may be capped at net par + 2 first — see the Net Double-Bogey Cap toggle below”. There is no toggle any more: the cap is always on. The note states it flat, once. Finding 11.

A fresh game every day

Irish Rumble is re-run each round, not accumulated over 36 holes: a new entry, a new pool and a new winning group every day. A golfer whose group has a bad Saturday starts level on Sunday.

Which is why this screen sets one day’s money. A two-day tournament runs it twice and settles it twice, and the leaderboard tab names the round.

A threesome borrows a 4th

Already built, and automatic: a group of three gets a borrowed 4th only when some other group has four — a 3×3×3 round has no asymmetry to fix, so nothing happens. The setup screen does not ask; it tells, which is the only thing missing today.

Donor poolEvery real golfer in every other group, in one fixed shuffled rotation. The card is spread across the field rather than shadowing one player.
HandicapThe donor’s own net, scored on the donor’s tee, so mixed tees and mixed gender come out right.
DonorsNever notified. Only the threesome sees the borrowed ball.
LagA donor who has not posted leaves the hole empty; the group shows a provisional total on three balls and firms up.

Called Borrowed 4th on every surface, never phantom — the leaderboard opens a donor-by-hole strip for a levelled group, and score entry carries a borrowed-ball banner.

A three-way split pays more each, and that is fine

Settled: the place splits among the real golfers in the winning group — $17.50 each in a four, $23.33 each in a three. The borrowed ball is not a person and cannot be paid, and nobody is going to argue that a threesome had the easier day: its fourth score arrives from a stranger it did not pick.

Ties split, and that is the rule

No countback, no arbitrary tiebreaker. Plenty of bets run on a given day, so a tie can be no action — tied foursomes split the place and the board says so. What the live build cannot do is split silently, under a chip that reads Winner takes all.

Field pool, foursome payout

The trap in these two games sitting next to each other: identical cards, different scopes. Irish Rumble is $10 × 7 in the field = $70 paid to a whole group, which splits among its real golfers — Match Play is $10 × 4 in one foursome paid to a golfer. Both facts are on the row that carries the money. Findings 9 and 10.

Still open

  • Whether Irish Rumble ever pays two places. Drawn at one, with the stepper live — a 12-golfer field might want 1st and 2nd.
  • Whether a tie that returns every entry should warn at settlement, since it is the one split that pays nobody anything.
  • Whether the segment preview should warn when a hole counts 4 balls and a group is levelled — that hole leans on the borrowed ball entirely.
