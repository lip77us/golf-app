How to play Triple Nassau

A Nassau for a threesome. Every golfer plays a separate Nassau against each of the other two, off one stake and one set of rules. Every hole you post counts in both of your matches.

THE SHORT ANSWER

Three one-on-one matches: Paul v Dave, Paul v Sam, Dave v Sam. Each one is a full Nassau, with a bet on the front nine, the back nine and the eighteen. You are in two of the matches, so you have six bets running. Strokes are worked out match by match, from the difference between the two handicaps.

The rules

Triple Nassau is for a group of three. Instead of two sides, there are three separate matches, one for every pair in the group. Each match is scored as match play, hole by hole, and each carries the three usual Nassau bets.

Nobody plays differently on the course. You post one score on each hole, and it goes into both of your matches. A result in one match has no effect on the other.

1
Three matches
Every golfer plays each of the other two. Paul v Dave, Paul v Sam and Dave v Sam.
2
Three bets each
Front nine, back nine and the eighteen, the same as any Nassau.
3
One setup
One stake, one handicap setting and one press rule, used by all three matches.
Why it suits a threesome
A two-sided Nassau needs even numbers. With three, somebody has to play alone against a pair or sit out. Here everyone is in two matches of their own, and the lowest handicap in the group gives strokes in both of his.

Strokes in each match

Each match is handicapped on its own. The lower handicap plays off scratch and the higher gets the difference, taken on the lowest stroke indexes. So a golfer can get strokes in one match and give them in the other.

1
Paul v Dave. Paul is 4 and Dave is 9, so Dave gets 5 strokes, on stroke index 1 to 5.
2
Paul v Sam. Sam is 14, so he gets 10 strokes from Paul, on stroke index 1 to 10.
3
Dave v Sam. Sam gets 5 from Dave, on stroke index 1 to 5. On the SI 8 hole, Sam gets a stroke against Paul and none against Dave.

The handicap setting is chosen once for all three matches: strokes off the low man, full net, or gross. An allowance below 100% is applied to each course handicap before the difference is taken.

Stakes and presses

The stake is per bet. At $10, each match is worth up to $30 before presses, and a golfer who loses all six bets owes $60: two matches, three bets each.

Presses belong to one match
A press starts a new bet over the rest of the current nine, in one match only. Going 2 down to Dave lets you press Dave. It does nothing to your match with Sam.
Manual or automatic
Presses can be called by the golfer who is behind, fired automatically at 2 down, or both. Only the golfer who is down can press.
A loss cap works per match
If you cap losses, the cap applies to each match separately. Your worst case across the round is twice the cap.

Questions people ask

How many bets are there in Triple Nassau?

Nine in total: three matches with three bets each. Each golfer is in two matches, so six of the nine involve him.

How do strokes work in Triple Nassau?

Each match is handicapped on its own. The lower handicap plays off scratch and the higher gets the difference on the lowest stroke indexes, so a golfer can get strokes against one opponent and give them to the other.

Can you press in Triple Nassau?

Yes. A press starts a new bet over the rest of the current nine and applies to one match only. Being 2 down to one golfer lets you press him and does not touch your other match.

What is the most I can lose in Triple Nassau?

Without presses, six times the stake: two matches with three bets each. With a loss cap, twice the cap, because the cap applies per match.

Can four golfers play Triple Nassau?

No. With four, play a standard Nassau as two against two, or a game built for four such as Wolf or Sixes.
