How to play Oakmont SWAT

A team game for a field of twelve or more. The teams are filled from the handicap list in snake order, so nobody plays strokes: every team plays its best gross ball, and every team plays every other team. The only hard part is an odd number of golfers, and the game has a rule for it.

THE SHORT ANSWER

The field is ordered by index and split into teams of four in snake order, which the committee can change, and each team plays as one group. A team’s score on a hole is its best gross ball. Every team plays every other team a Nassau: front, back and overall. When the field doesn’t divide by four, the short teams play as threesomes with a phantom fourth ball, and their golfers play for 4/3 the stake so every team has the same on each bet.

The rules

Twelve or more golfers, gross scores. The handicap index does one job, which is to set the starting teams. After that nobody gets a stroke: the snake order has already balanced the sides, and each team’s score on a hole is simply its best ball.

Every pair of teams plays a match-play Nassau on those best balls: a front nine, a back nine and an overall eighteen, each its own bet. In a field of four teams that’s six matches and eighteen bets. A tied bet pays nothing — no countback, nothing rolls forward.

Matches only count shared holes
The teams are on the course at different times, so during the round a match only stands as far as both teams have played. Team Lipkin through 11 against Team Brandt through 10 is a match through 10. Nothing is settled on a hole until both teams have it.

The teams

Order the field by Handicap Index, lowest first, and count the teams: the field divided by four, rounded up. Then deal the list out in snake order: one golfer to each team from team 1 to the last, then back from the last to team 1, then out again, then back.

The snake is what makes it fair. The team that gets the best golfer gets the last of the second pass and the first of the third, so the teams come out close without anyone handing out strokes. It’s a starting point, not a draft: nobody picks, and the committee can change it. Each team is named for its lowest index and plays together as a group.

Ties on the list
Two golfers on the same index go in order of course handicap, then surname. It only decides which team each starts on.

Changing the teams
The committee can move or swap anyone: to split up a regular pairing, keep a cart together or even up a team that looks strong. Every team has to finish with three or four, or five where the course allows groups of five. A fivesome plays its best of five, has no phantom, and its golfers play for 4/5 of the stake.

Why twelve
Three full teams, before any threesome.
Twelve is the smallest field with three full teams. With nine to eleven, most of the teams would be threesomes leaning on someone else’s phantom, and the round robin would be three matches. Below twelve, play something else: a four-ball Nassau or Irish Rumble suits a smaller field.

Threesomes and the phantom

When the field doesn’t divide by four, the last pass of the snake runs out before every team has a fourth. Those teams start as threesomes: one team in a field of 15, two in 14, three in 13. Because the last pass runs backwards, they’re the teams with the best golfers. A threesome’s fourth ball is a phantom: on each hole, the gross score of one of the bottom golfers, the highest index on each foursome (or fivesome), drawn at random for each hole and never named. So the missing player is replaced by the kind of golfer the snake would have given the team, and if the committee changes the teams, the bottom golfers change with them.

Three rules keep it honest. The phantom is never named, and whose score it takes is shuffled at random, so nobody can look it up on the leaderboard. Its score is hidden from the threesome until its three golfers have finished the hole, so nobody plays the hole knowing what the phantom made. And the threesome’s golfers play for 4/3 of a foursome golfer’s stake: every bet is team against team at one team stake, and three golfers split it three ways. At $6 a foursome golfer, a threesome golfer has $8 on each bet.

Worth agreeing before the groups go out

The game itself has few settings. These are the questions a committee gets asked.

Which index?
The current Handicap Index on the day. It only sets the starting order, so a tenth either way changes at most which team a golfer starts on.
Why gross?
Because the snake order has already done the handicapping. Every team has a player from each band of the list, so the sides are level before a shot is hit, and best ball gross is the simplest thing to score and the hardest to argue about.
How much can the day cost?
Three bets a match and a match against every other team. With four teams and $6 a golfer, a foursome golfer has at most $54 at risk and a threesome golfer $72. With six teams it’s five matches, so pick the stake for the size of the field.
What if someone withdraws?
Decide it before the first tee: fill the teams again from the list if the field changes before play, or let the short team play as a threesome with the phantom if it changes after the groups go out.

Scoring SWAT without doing the arithmetic

One Nassau is easy. Six of them across four groups is not: every match depends on a team that is on a different hole, a threesome’s score depends on a golfer in another group, and the money is split in quarters for some golfers and thirds for others.

Questions people ask

Do the threesomes have an advantage?

No. The phantom is a bottom golfer’s score, the same kind of ball the snake would have given them, and it only counts on a hole when it beats all three of theirs. And the money is level: each team has the same on every bet, the threesome’s golfers just carry a third of it each.

How are the teams made?

List the golfers by handicap index, lowest first. Give the first golfer on the list to team 1, the next to team 2, and so on to the last team; then come back the other way, last team to team 1, and keep snaking until the list runs out. Each team ends up with one golfer from every band of the list, and the committee can move anyone from there.

What happens with an odd number of players?

The teams the draft can’t fill play as threesomes. Their fourth ball is a phantom that takes a bottom golfer’s score, drawn at random for each hole, never named, and hidden until the threesome has finished the hole, and their golfers play for 4/3 of the stake so each team has the same money on every bet.

Why do threesome golfers pay more?

Because the bets are between teams. Every team puts the same amount on every bet; a foursome splits it four ways and a threesome three ways. So a threesome golfer pays more when his team loses and collects more when it wins, and neither side of a match is playing for less. A fivesome, where the course allows one, is the other way round: five golfers split the same stake, $4.80 each at $6.
