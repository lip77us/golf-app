How to play Alternate Shot

Two partners, one ball, hitting in turn until it’s holed. Also called foursomes. The hardest pairs format, and the best one to watch.

THE SHORT ANSWER

Partners take turns hitting the same ball. One tees off on the odd holes and the other on the even holes, whoever holed the last putt. One score a hole, and the pair plays off half its combined handicap.

The rules

1
One ball
The pair plays one ball on every hole.
2
Take turns
Partners hit it in turn until it is holed. Nobody hits twice in a row.
3
Odd and even tees
One partner tees off on every odd hole, the other on every even hole, whoever finished the hole before.

A penalty stroke doesn’t change the order. If Mike hits it in the water, the penalty is added and Tom plays the next shot.

Who tees

Decide on the 1st tee who takes the odd holes, and it is fixed for all 18. The decision matters: the odd-hole golfer usually gets more of the par 5s and the long par 4s, so look at the card first.

Handicap

The pair plays off 50% of the two course handicaps added together, the most generous allowance of any pairs format. One ball means the low man only hits half the shots.

The same two golfers get three times the strokes in alternate shot that they get in a scramble, purely from the format. Every figure is worked from course handicap, rounded once on the total, half up.

As a match

Two pairs in one group can play it hole by hole, as the Ryder Cup does on its foursomes mornings. The lower pair score wins the hole, a tie halves it, and the match closes out like any other.

Halved scores it as a two-on-two match in one group, with one score a pair on every hole. Foursomes is also one of the three segments of Triple Cup.

The money

Entry and the pool
Every golfer pays the same entry into one pool. The organiser sets how many places pay, from one to four, and the split.
Paid to the pair
Each place pays the pair, and the prize divides two ways.
Ties
Tied pairs split the places they cover.

Questions people ask

How does alternate shot work in golf?

Two partners play one ball and take turns hitting it. One tees off on the odd holes and the other on the even holes.

Is alternate shot the same as foursomes?

Yes. Foursomes is the traditional name, used in the Ryder Cup. Halved calls the pairs format Alternate Shot.

Who tees off first in alternate shot?

The pair decides on the 1st tee. That golfer tees off on every odd hole and his partner on every even hole.

What handicap do you get in alternate shot?

50% of the two partners’ course handicaps added together, rounded once.

Can two pairs in one group play alternate shot as a match?

Yes. Each pair plays one ball in turn, and the lower pair score wins the hole. Halved keeps the match for you.

Does a penalty stroke change who hits next?

No. The penalty is added to the score, and the partner who didn’t hit the last shot plays next.
