How to play Stroke Play

Every stroke counts, and the lowest total wins. Play it gross, net, or both from the same cards. Every tournament is built on it.

THE SHORT ANSWER

Count every stroke on all 18 holes. The lowest total wins low gross. Take off your handicap strokes, and the lowest of those totals wins low net. One set of cards, two boards.

The rules

Every golfer plays his own ball and holes out on every hole. There is no opponent on the hole, only the card.

1
Hole out
Every stroke goes on the card, penalty strokes too. Nothing is conceded.
2
Add it up
Your gross score is the total of the 18 holes.
3
Lowest wins
Low gross is the lowest total. Low net is the lowest total after your handicap strokes come off.

It is the fairest test there is and the least forgiving. One big number stays on the card, which is why most groups play it with a cap on every hole.

Net scoring

Your course handicap is how many strokes you get over 18 holes. They go on the holes in stroke-index order, hardest first. A 14 gets one stroke on each of the 14 hardest holes. A 22 gets one on every hole and a second on the four hardest.

Full handicap
Net stroke play normally uses 100% of each golfer’s course handicap. The organiser can set a lower allowance for the whole field.
Strokes off the low
Inside one group, the lowest handicap plays to scratch and everyone else gets the difference. Same order, smaller numbers.
Gross
No strokes at all. The real card is the score.

The net double bogey cap

Without a cap, a 10 on one hole usually ends a round. With the cap on, no hole counts for more than net double bogey: par, plus two, plus any strokes you get on that hole.

It is the same limit the World Handicap System uses when you post a score. In Halved, a casual round uses it at full net when the round has it switched on. An individual tournament always caps the net board, at any allowance. The gross board is never capped: it shows the real card.

More than one round

Over two or more days, the totals add up. The lowest net over every round wins the championship, and each day’s board stands on its own as well.

In a big field with a wide spread of handicaps, many clubs split the field into flights by index, so the 5s and the 25s play for separate prizes.

Ties

Halved splits ties. Tied golfers share the prize for the places they cover, with no countback. Two golfers tied for first in a field paying two places split first and second between them.

Some clubs break ties by matching cards instead: best back nine, then the last six, the last three and the last hole. Halved doesn’t do that, so if your club does, agree it before you tee off.

Questions people ask

How does stroke play work in golf?

Every golfer counts every stroke on all 18 holes, and the lowest total wins. In net stroke play, each golfer’s handicap strokes come off his total first.

What is the difference between stroke play and match play?

Stroke play counts every stroke over the round. Match play is won hole by hole: a 9 on one hole only loses that hole.

How are handicap strokes given in net stroke play?

You get your course handicap in strokes, one a hole on the hardest holes by stroke index. A handicap over 18 gets a second stroke on the hardest holes.

What is net double bogey?

Par plus two plus any handicap strokes you get on the hole. With the cap on, nothing worse counts.

How are ties settled in stroke play?

In Halved, tied golfers split the prize for the places they cover, with no countback. Some clubs match cards instead, starting with the back nine.

Can stroke play run alongside another game?

Yes. In Halved, Stroke Play can ride along with a match game on the same scores, as a leaderboard of its own.
